01

shuohao-skills

Worth studyingOpen-source traction 655

Turns a novel into a shoot-ready short-drama kit: character bibles, look prompts, voice profiles, episode outlines.

The old behavior is unmistakable — a writer hand-writing character bios, an art lead producing reference sheets, a director breaking it into episodes. Three to five people, one to two weeks.

Why it's worth your time: while everyone fights over generating footage, it walked upstream to preparing the material. That stretch eats the most people, leans hardest on experience, and is the least likely to get productized, because it demos badly. The part to steal is its 13 quality gates — turning creative quality into hard constraints a script can check, before anyone looks at the work.

Revenue is open source as the hook, a paid community as the till. Low ceiling, but the path already works.

02

open-ai-canvas

Worth studyingOpen-source traction 535

One canvas holds a whole production: pull character assets out of a novel, then arrange storyboards, images, video, and audio into a film.

It's a bet on the container, not the generator. It builds no model of its own — only the canvas that organizes what other models produce. The logic underneath: model quality converges, and what's left to compete on is who owns the structure of the work.

Go read its character asset schema. How it defines a character's look, voice, and temperament and holds those consistent across separate generation steps is the hardest problem in any multi-step creative pipeline, and someone has published an answer.

⚠️ One flag: the project homepage points at ddcat.pronhubcn.com, and the shape of that domain is suspicious. Don't upload material there until it's explained.

03

The GTM Co-Founder

Keep watchingOpen-source traction 176

A marketing brain for technical founders who don't have a co-founder.

It's trying to package judgment as a tool, which is the hardest thing on this list. What it can do: the order of the questions, which metrics to watch, the list of traps. What it can't do: tell you which path fits your specific situation.

That line is the useful part. If you're productizing expertise, it's your pricing boundary — give away the standardizable half, charge for the judgment.

Also read its opening line. It doesn't say "we provide GTM solutions," it says "some days are just plain hard." That, not the feature set, is what took it to number one for the day.

SaaS is being rebuilt for agents

Not a guess — this is what showed up in a single day:

Product What it is
Comp AI CRM A CRM for AI agents
Docker Sandboxes Disposable isolated sandboxes for agents, from Docker itself
OptMem Permanent memory for agents
axern Open-source sandboxes for agents
numbat Observability for agent behavior
Reference Local semantic search for agents
Toolport One MCP configuration shared across all your agents
mcp-server A working phone number for an agent
Progress AI Observability Tracing and evaluation for agents in production

CRM, search, sandboxes, memory, observability — even a phone number. All of it, in one day.

What it means: the money right now is flowing into tools sold to agents, not applications sold to people. If you're building an AI product for humans, this pile-up isn't your competition. It's evidence that the human-facing end is being relatively ignored.
