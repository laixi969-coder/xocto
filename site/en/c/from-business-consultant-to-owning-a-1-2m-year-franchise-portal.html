Real-demand verdict

Real-demand verdict

Real demand

Franchisors have a standing budget for franchisee acquisition, and the portal saves them from buying traffic and qualifying leads themselves; the story cites about 1,000 clients and 675K monthly visitors, suggesting both sides keep showing up.

How it makes money

Franchisors pay to list their brands on the portals and receive prospective-franchisee leads; revenue comes from client subscriptions and lead services, though the exact pricing structure isn't disclosed.

What old behavior it replaces

Franchisors buying their own ads, attending expos, and relying on word of mouth to recruit franchisees.

02

Where the first customers came from

Not stated for the 1998 launch; current acquisition relies on inbound client inquiries, email marketing and remarketing, franchise expos, and referrals.

03

Tactics you can copy

  1. 01Split into country- and language-specific portals, each running its own SEO, to cover multiple markets with one playbook.
  2. 02Treat the lead form and homepage as a continuous experiment: daily A/B tests and tweaks, with lead quality as the retention lever.
  3. 03Build the email list as an asset: the story cites 500K+ active subscribers, up 17% year over year, used for both acquisition and lead distribution.
Moving it to an AI business

This is a classic internet lead-gen business, not AI-native. What transfers is the structure: a vertical lead portal, multi-site SEO, and daily form experiments. For an AI product, treat it as a demand-validation and lead-distribution layer rather than the product itself.
