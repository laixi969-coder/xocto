On Launching A Technology That Turns Photos Into Coloring Books -

Real demand

A Chicago company converts user photos into coloring pages and custom coloring books, selling digital and physical products to parents, teachers and therapists, and licensing the books to hotels and resorts as marketing collateral.

Real-demand verdict

Real-demand verdict

Real demand

It replaces the manual tracing of a photo into line art, and parents, teachers and autism or Alzheimer's therapists use it repeatedly; placement in major retail chains shows people keep paying.

How it makes money

Customers pay per coloring page or custom book; physical books and gift cards sell through retail chains like Toys R Us, BJ's and Sam's Club, while businesses buy books in bulk for marketing and fundraising.

What old behavior it replaces

Hand-tracing a photo into line art, or buying pre-made cartoon coloring books.

03

Tactics you can copy

  1. 01Split one image-processing capability into two lines: per-page or per-book sales to consumers, and bulk sales to hotels, camps and bakeries as marketing collateral.
  2. 02Use patents to fence off the core conversion technology as a moat against copycats.
  3. 03Get the product onto shelves at chains like Toys R Us and Sam's Club to reach parents who never search online.
Moving it to an AI business

For AI founders: packaging a generation capability as a physical or printable artifact is easier for non-technical buyers to grasp than a subscription; selling the same model to consumers and to businesses needing marketing collateral is a concrete way to raise revenue per customer.
