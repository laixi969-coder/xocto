Real-demand verdict

Real-demand verdict

Real demand

It replaces a concrete old behavior: men raiding the women's aisle for ties that snap and pull hair. At least one sale every day after launch, orders from all 50 US states and 50 countries in year one, and growth to $30K/month in four years point to sustained payment from an unserved niche.

How it makes money

Men with long hair—served by nothing but flimsy women's ties—buy purpose-designed hair ties direct from the brand's own online store, through pre-orders and everyday e-commerce retail.

What old behavior it replaces

Low-quality hair ties designed and marketed exclusively for women, which snapped and pulled hair.

02

Where the first customers came from

A year of weekly blogging built the first readers and email subscribers; the product launched via a pre-order party, after which organic Google and YouTube searches like "awkward stage hair" and "how to tie your hair for men" brought daily first orders.

03

Tactics you can copy

  1. 01Sell content before product: publish one article or video a week for your niche starting a full year before launch, never missing, turning search demand into an audience you own.
  2. 02Claim unclaimed long-tail search terms: build content around zero-competition queries like "awkward stage hair"—one early hit still drives 15K unique visitors a month.
  3. 03Run an advocate/educate/celebrate content mix: tutorials solve real problems, celebration posts feature user submissions, turning readers into contributors and repeat buyers (42K+ blog comments).
Moving it to an AI business

Not an AI product, but the playbook moves cleanly: find a niche full of unanswered questions, capture that demand first with content or a small AI tool, then sell a product purpose-built for that audience. In the AI-search era, the same positioning play becomes getting cited by AI assistants.
