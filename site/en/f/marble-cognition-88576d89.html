FUNDING DESK · Germany · Enterprise process discovery and AI workflow automation (governance layer for AI coworkers)

Marble Cognition

Marble Cognition is a Munich-based AI company that records employees' everyday work across tools, maps processes and ranks automation potential, then deploys human-approved AI coworkers.

Business judgment

01

What the product does

Company-reported product flow is Capture, Understand, Identify, Automate and Measure: Marble records work on the employee's local machine across tabs, legacy tools, VPNs and virtual desktops, turns actions into a process map, ranks processes by complexity and automation potential, tests AI coworkers on real cases, and keeps human approval where judgment matters. The homepage shows redaction of names, addresses and account numbers in an invoice-approval screen, and claims SOC 2 certification, optional EU hosting and the ability to run full anonymization in the customer's own environment. The product page lists use cases in customer support, finance, insurance claims and commissions, accounts receivable, payment matching and private-equity integration, but these are scenario lists rather than delivered case studies.

02

Users, buyers and demand

Users are frontline teams drowning in tabs and repetitive work: support, finance and accounting, insurance claims and commissions, professional-services receivables and month-end closing, and financial-services payment matching. The payer is more likely a function head or operations/transformation lead, because the product promises per-process and per-team measurement of hours saved and approval rates. Pain frequency is high: refunds, invoice matching and payment runs are daily work with exception branches, exactly where process discovery matters. Public material does not identify buyer profiles, team sizes or purchase triggers.

03

The actual workflow

Employees switch recording on themselves; Marble captures cross-tool actions, consolidates them into processes with edge cases, and scores automation potential (the page shows refund handling at 86% and invoice matching at 79%). AI coworkers are then tested on real historical cases; low-risk cases are auto-approved while cases above a threshold (the example is refunds over EUR 500) go to a person. Finally, Marble reports hours saved, cases handled and approval-without-edit rates per process and team, and generates SOPs that update as work changes. Inputs are screen actions and business documents; outputs are process maps, auditable AI execution and impact reports.

04

Pricing and unit economics

The input evidence does not disclose pricing, billing unit (seat, process or volume), trial terms or implementation fees, so unit economics cannot be assessed. An inference, not a company disclosure: sales look enterprise-oriented, requiring a local capture agent, security review and process-mapping services, with onboarding measured in weeks, likely a subscription-plus-implementation mix. Volume-based pricing would grow revenue with migration depth but depend more heavily on verifiable ROI reporting.

05

Adoption evidence and gaps

Confirmed adoption evidence is limited to a pre-seed round with Heartcore Capital and Seedcamp and the company's own industry and use-case descriptions; no customer count, paid contracts, retention, processing volume or revenue is public. Figures such as 4,828 refunds handled in a day and a 50% approval-without-edit rate are product-interface illustrations, not audited customer results. Missing case pages, documentation and pricing leave product maturity and deployability unverified.

06

Competition and defensibility

Versus general models and agent builders, Marble's difference is knowing the organization's real processes and exceptions first, then constraining and evaluating AI execution against them, with approval, audit and redaction built into capture; versus old workflows, it turns tacit knowledge into a continuously updated process asset. The moat holds only if capture across legacy systems and virtual desktops is reliable, redaction and compliance survive enterprise review, process maps stay accurate, and switching costs rise as processes accumulate. It weakens if model vendors offer enterprise process discovery directly or if customers find screen capture too intrusive.

07

How to read this round

This is a pre-seed round, indicating a product and early-customer validation stage; the capital supports work on capture, governance and deployment. The investor list is not a lead-investor list, and amount and currency follow the deterministic layer. Use of funds is undisclosed, so no specific allocation can be inferred. The next milestone should be publicly verifiable paid case studies, deployment timelines and process-level ROI data, not more scenario lists.

08

Where it could fail

First, employee screen recording is privacy- and labor-sensitive; if redaction or consent mechanisms are challenged in real deployments, security and legal reviews can block sales. Second, if process discovery cannot reliably cover legacy systems and exception branches, automation-potential scores will be wrong and pilots will not expand. Third, without proof of process-level savings and approval quality, buyers may treat it as another AI tool shelved after a trial; opaque pricing and implementation costs can also lengthen sales cycles.

09

What you can take from it

A transferable practice is productizing 'understand the work before automating it': build a process baseline from real actions rather than interviews, test AI on historical cases, keep human approval at high-risk steps, and make savings and approval rates auditable. For founders, governance and measurability can be a differentiator, but interface illustrations must quickly be replaced by paid-customer data.

10

What to watch next

Watch for published pricing and deployment documentation; named customer cases with before-and-after process metrics; capture-agent reliability on virtual desktops and VPNs; and disclosure of paid customer counts, expansion rates and changes in human-approval share.
