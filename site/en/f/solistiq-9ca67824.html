FUNDING DESK · Germany · Mid-to-large manufacturing, branded-goods, and trading companies that manage wide SKU portfolios with tiered pricing, minimum-order-quantity constraints, and multi-supplier lead-time variability.

SOLISTIQ

SOLISTIQ builds Q-Foundry, a domain-specific foundation model for demand forecasting, and Agent Q, an explainable auto-replenishment layer, targeting industrial and commercial companies' SKU-level inventory and ordering decisions.

Work it replaces

Manual reorder-point and ABC-classification rules in legacy WMS/ERP, plus the purchasing-manager experience loop of judging order quantities and safety stock category by category.

Business judgment

If the company-reported 24 % forecast-error reduction and 15–25 % inventory reduction hold in heterogeneous client portfolios, SOLISTIQ addresses a high-frequency, quantifiable pain point in working capital; however, both figures remain self-reported with no third-party audit or published case study to corroborate them.

01

What the product does

Q-Foundry is pre-trained on 2.5 million products and over 1.5 billion data points (company-reported); it captures trends, seasonality, and outliers, and ingests external signals such as weather, holidays, and search-trend data. Downstream, Agent Q converts forecasts into concrete order suggestions that jointly optimise tiered pricing, minimum quantities, storage costs, and availability targets, with a human-approval step. The model is developed in Germany and runs on SOLISTIQ's own hardware in Würzburg, positioned as an EU-sovereignty offering. Current scope ends at order recommendation; procurement contracting, supplier substitution, and cross-warehouse transfer are not documented.

02

Users, buyers and demand

Day-to-day users are inventory-planning or purchasing teams inside a single enterprise; the economic buyer is likely an operations or finance director responsible for working capital. The pain recurs daily—every SKU requires a make/order/no-order decision, and a wrong call translates immediately into either stockout loss or excess carrying cost. No named customer logos or case studies appear on the site; adoption evidence is absent.

03

The actual workflow

Onboarding requires a one-time export of sales/shipping data; no implementation project is needed. A free proof-of-concept (company-reported, ≈ 1 month) replays the client's last several months day-by-day: (1) stock and open deliveries at day t; (2) Q-Foundry outputs forecast and uncertainty band; (3) Agent Q factors availability target, lead time, MOQ, packaging, and cost to produce a specific order quantity; (4) goods arrive after lead time; (5) actual demand at day t either depletes stock or results in lost sales; the cycle repeats at t+1. The user reviews Agent Q's explanation and approves or overrides. 80–90 % automation is a company-stated target, not a measured figure.

04

Pricing and unit economics

The PoC is free (company-reported). Production pricing, subscription tiers, per-SKU billing, or hardware-licence structures are not published. Unit-economics inference is conditional: if inference runs on owned hardware at a marginal cost that scales linearly with SKU count, gross margin structure may differ from a pure cloud-AI SaaS; no data currently supports or refutes this.

05

Adoption evidence and gaps

The only confirmed signal is the Pre-Seed financing itself and the funnel design around a free one-week PoC. The 2.5 M-product / 1.5 B-datapoint training corpus is company-reported; its provenance (proprietary, partner, public) is unstated. No revenue, ARR, client count, or third-party validation is available in the captured evidence.

06

Competition and defensibility

Versus generic LLMs applied to forecasting, SOLISTIQ's differentiation rests on (1) domain pre-training depth—1.5 B inventory-specific data points baked into Q-Foundry; (2) EU data-sovereignty positioning with on-premises hardware; (3) multi-factor joint optimisation (MOQ, tiered pricing, storage cost) rather than single-metric forecast accuracy. The moat is conditional: if a general model plus light fine-tuning achieves comparable precision and client data-residency requirements relax, the differentiation narrows quickly.

07

How to read this round

The company labels the round Pre-Seed on its site; the funding record lists stage as 'Other.' Investors include individual angels (Kohrmann, Seelig, Metzger, Dubosq) and funds Superangels and May Ventures; no explicit lead is identified. Use of proceeds is undisclosed. The stage signals that model pre-training and PoC workflow are complete; the next milestone is almost certainly first paid-client contracts and revenue recognition.

08

Where it could fail

1) If the 24 % error reduction and 15–25 % inventory reduction are achievable only in favourable data regimes, heterogeneous client portfolios with high-turnover, long-tail, or heavy-promotion SKUs may see materially lower ROI, depressing PoC-to-paid conversion. 2) Established players (Kinaxis, o9, Blue Yonder, and German-local tools such as ALEAS) bundle AI forecasting inside broader supply-chain suites; without end-to-end workflow lock-in, SOLISTIQ risks being commoditised as a forecasting API bolted on to incumbent WMS platforms.

09

What you can take from it

Picking a daily, high-frequency task whose error is immediately measurable in inventory days or stockout cost—then pairing a domain foundation model with a zero-friction free PoC (one-week result, no implementation project)—is a repeatable pattern for AI founders who need to prove value before asking for a long sales cycle.

10

What to watch next

Whether 3+ named paid customers with citable inventory/stockout-improvement numbers appear within two quarters; whether Q-Foundry is exposed via API or embedded in existing WMS versus a standalone SaaS; and whether owned-hardware inference marginal cost scales linearly with SKU count, which will determine whether the pricing model can sustain gross margins.
