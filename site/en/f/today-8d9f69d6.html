TODAY

TODAY is an AI workspace for financial and insurance advisors that combines conversation transcription, compliance documentation, CRM sync, sales coaching and AI simulation training in one product.

Work it replaces

Manual IDD advisory records, duplicate CRM entry after meetings, one-to-one mentor role-plays and ad hoc sales reviews that depend on individual experience.

Business judgment

The key commercial question is whether TODAY can prove retention and onboarding efficiency inside regulated sales organisations, rather than selling generic meeting notes; its funding story is stronger than its publicly verifiable paid-adoption evidence.

01

What the product does

The company site describes four capabilities: background transcription with summaries and IDD-compliant advisory documentation; extraction of data points into CRM or broker systems; per-conversation scoring and phrasing feedback aligned to an organisation's advisory logic; and configurable AI clients for simulation training. TODAY Voice handles inbound calls, appointments and pre-qualification, but the pricing page states that Simulations and Voice are not part of self-service plans and are calibrated with the vendor. The scope is broad, yet public material is mostly marketing pages and one case study; detailed documentation, model boundaries and error handling are not disclosed.

02

Users, buyers and demand

Users are insurance agents, brokers, team leads and training managers; buyers are typically sales organisations, tied agencies or financial distributors with hundreds of advisors. The pain is heavy post-meeting administration, time-consuming compliance records, mentor time consumed by onboarding, and inconsistent conversation standards across teams. The Königswege case describes an organisation with 1,800 sales partners, indicating a high-frequency, multi-person, process-heavy target, while individual advisors can also subscribe monthly.

03

The actual workflow

An advisor starts TODAY during a video, in-person or VoIP conversation; the system streams the transcript and recognises technical terms, tariffs and insurer names. After the meeting it produces a summary, follow-ups and IDD documentation, extracts data points such as salary, family and policies into the CRM, then scores the conversation on opening, structure, objection handling and cross-selling with suggested phrasing. New advisors practise with AI clients and receive feedback against the same scorecards. The site claims documentation is created in about 12 seconds and that roughly 30-plus data points are extracted per conversation; these are company-reported figures.

04

Pricing and unit economics

The pricing page lists Pro at €29 per advisor per month and Performance at €79, both plus VAT, with 12% annual savings and free manager seats on Performance; Enterprise is custom from 10 advisors and includes SSO, audit logs and on-premise or regional cloud options. A four-week free trial and money-back guarantee are offered with no setup fee. Simulations and Voice are quoted separately and their prices are not disclosed. The site's claim that an advisor saves about 30 minutes per conversation implies plausible unit economics, but this is a company-reported inference; renewal, gross margin and acquisition cost data are missing.

05

Adoption evidence and gaps

The site claims more than 3,500 advisors use TODAY and presents a Königswege case with 80 hours of onboarding effort saved per month, 235 partners completing at least one simulation, 1,690 simulations in total and a 23.5% average score improvement. All of these are company-reported and unaudited. The funding record only confirms that TODAY develops AI tools for financial advisors; it provides no revenue, retention or paid-conversion data. The case-study page has one full story and several 'coming soon' placeholders, so adoption evidence remains thin.

06

Competition and defensibility

Compared with generic transcription or foundation models, TODAY's differentiation lies in insurance terminology, IDD document templates, CRM connectors, organisation-specific scorecards and configurable simulation personas, all of which require industry knowledge and customer co-design. The moat depends on compliance and data-residency requirements becoming procurement gates, scorecards being trusted as a measure of sales quality, and switching costs after organisation-wide deployment. If general model vendors add insurance templates, or if advisors reject the coaching scores, the advantage narrows quickly.

07

How to read this round

This is a seed round; the amount and investor list are rendered by the deterministic layer, and the investor list is not a lead-investor list. The financing signals investor appetite for AI workflows inside regulated sales organisations, but use of funds is undisclosed, so it cannot be inferred whether the money goes to sales, model development or compliance certification. The next milestone should be verifiable paid customer counts, net revenue retention, the share of Pro customers upgrading to Performance, and commercial progress on Simulations and Voice.

08

Where it could fail

First, compliance and data risk: the product processes health, income and family information, and an incomplete or incorrect AI document could create IDD liability and regulatory exposure. Second, product-credibility risk: if advisors see coaching scores as arbitrary, adoption will fall; if simulations diverge from real clients, renewal rationale weakens. Third, commercial risk: the low-cost self-service tier is easy for general tools to undercut, while Enterprise sales cycles and customisation costs may slow growth.

09

What you can take from it

TODAY's lesson is to embed AI in a high-frequency, compliance-heavy, measurable workflow rather than sell a general chatbot; use documentation time savings as the entry point, then expand into coaching and simulation. For founders, securing one anchor customer willing to co-design scorecards is more valuable than broad, shallow distribution.

10

What to watch next

Watch how many of the 3,500 advisors are paying and active; whether full case studies appear beyond Königswege; the upgrade ratio from Pro to Performance; and the separate pricing and adoption of Simulations and Voice.
