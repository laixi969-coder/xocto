Work it replaces

Replaces manual design-to-code pipelines for mobile apps, outsourced prototyping, and multi-role App Store release workflows.

Business judgment

Bilt compresses the native mobile development toolchain into a prompt-to-publishable-app workflow, but its commercial test is whether non-technical users can repeatedly ship operable, monetizable apps rather than just prototypes.

01

What the product does

Bilt's core capability is converting natural-language descriptions, screenshots, or documents into runnable native mobile apps, automatically generating frontend, database, authentication, and storage components. The site states it supports importing Figma projects, converting existing web apps to mobile, and handling build, signing, and submission to the Apple App Store and Google Play. Enterprise features include design systems, team collaboration, white-labeling, SSO/SCIM, encryption, and API/CI-CD access. The product spans idea-to-store, but the quality boundaries of generated code, platform compatibility, and long-term maintainability are not quantified in public materials.

02

Users, buyers and demand

Target users include non-technical founders, indie developers, product teams, and enterprise innovation groups. Testimonials on the site come from CTOs, CEOs, founders, a security engineer, and solopreneurs, with pain points around long mobile development cycles, tedious design-to-code pipelines, slow prototype validation, and teams tied up on core products. Payers may be individual developers on monthly subscriptions or enterprise customers on custom contracts. However, the site does not disclose customer counts, retention, or enterprise client names; adoption evidence is mainly company-reported testimonials.

03

The actual workflow

Users start by describing an app idea in natural language or uploading screenshots, documents, or Figma projects. Bilt generates the app with live preview, letting users view it on their phone and iterate with feedback. They then adjust features, configure backend and authentication, and publish to the App Store or Google Play through the platform. Enterprise plans also integrate with existing CI/CD pipelines, version control, and custom deployment targets. The flow compresses traditional multi-role collaboration into a single prompt-driven loop, though actual store review, compliance, and ongoing maintenance still depend on the user.

04

Pricing and unit economics

The pricing page shows a free plan with 3M monthly AI tokens and paid plans starting at €15/month (annual discount). Token tiers include Professional at 10M tokens for €25/month or €19/month annually; Professional Plus at 20M tokens for €50/month or €39/month annually; Professional Max at 45M tokens for €100/month or €79/month annually; and Professional Ultra at 115M tokens for €250/month or €195/month annually. One-time token top-ups are available, with 40% off on paid plans. Enterprise is custom-quoted with SLA, dedicated support, and data residency options. Unit economics are not disclosed; the relationship between token consumption and AI inference cost is a key inferred variable.

05

Adoption evidence and gaps

The site displays testimonials from users at Buildwill, Alpakids, Leio, PostSlate, and others, claiming prototypes built in one session, full-stack apps generated in minutes, and no technical expertise required. The company also cites RevenueCat's 2025 State of Subscription Apps report noting over $10B earned by subscription apps, 12% of downloads converting to paying customers, and $8,880/month for the top 5% of new apps—but these are industry figures, not Bilt's own performance. On funding, the company raised a $700K pre-seed round from Superhero Capital, Markus Villig, and Martin Sokk, with proceeds for global expansion and product development. Customer counts, revenue, retention, and actual published app numbers are undisclosed.

06

Competition and defensibility

Bilt's potential moat lies in integrating native mobile build, signing, publishing, and full-stack backend into a single prompt-driven flow, which is closer to the last mile of mobile app delivery than generic code-generation models. Enterprise security and compliance features (SSO, SCIM, encryption, zero retention) and CI/CD integration can raise switching costs for teams. However, as general large models improve, underlying code generation may commoditize; Bilt needs to build durable advantage through publishing pipelines, enterprise compliance, and design-system integration. There is no public evidence yet on the reliability of its generated code in complex production environments or user switching costs.

07

How to read this round

This is a pre-seed round of $700K from Superhero Capital and two angel investors. Such rounds typically validate product-market fit and build an early team; the stated use of proceeds is global expansion and product development, but the specific allocation is unknown. The next milestone should be proving that non-technical users can continuously publish and operate apps, not just generate prototypes, while disclosing customer adoption, retention, or revenue data to support a follow-on round.

08

Where it could fail

First, if generated apps fail App Store review requirements or production-grade stability, users will drop off at the publishing stage, undermining the value proposition. Second, if the unit economics between AI token costs and subscription pricing are unbalanced, the free plan and lower paid tiers may not cover inference costs. Third, general models and large cloud vendors launching similar mobile app generation features could compress Bilt's differentiation. Fourth, enterprise sales cycles are long and compliance-heavy; without proven security certifications and data residency capabilities, enterprise revenue will be hard to scale.

09

What you can take from it

Bilt's lesson is to embed AI capability into a clear delivery endpoint—a native app publishable to app stores—rather than stopping at code snippets or prototypes. For AI product founders, binding generation to downstream workflows like publishing, compliance, and collaboration can build more stickiness than improving generation quality alone. Meanwhile, token-based pricing must be finely matched to inference costs, or the free tier may become a burden.

10

What to watch next

Verifiable next indicators include: number of paying users or enterprise customers, number of apps actually published to app stores, user retention and token consumption distribution, and whether public customer case studies emerge. Also watch for disclosed revenue or follow-on funding, and progress on enterprise security certifications such as SOC 2.
