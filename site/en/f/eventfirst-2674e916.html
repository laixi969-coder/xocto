FUNDING DESK · · B2B corporate event full-service + compliance budget platform (not consumer, not event-calendar SaaS).

eventfirst

eventfirst is an all-in-one AI platform for B2B corporate events that integrates venue/supplier sourcing, comparative RFPs, approval workflows, budget compliance, participant management, and on-site execution coordination into a single workflow.

Work it replaces

The full manual chain used by in-house event ops teams: from briefing to multi-supplier price comparison (venues, catering, AV, ground transport), contract negotiation, approval routing, participant RSVP and rooming-list management; plus the procurement department's per-supplier onboarding and maverick-spend tracking.

Business judgment

The core bet is that LLM-driven structured RFP and supplier matching compress 30+ manual steps traditionally handled by event brokers and procurement teams into a 24–72-hour binding-quote cycle; if the supplier network and approval engine keep scaling, eventfirst becomes a data-flywheel platform with transaction-based revenue; otherwise it risks collapsing into a premium event-outsourcing service with thin margins.

01

What the product does

The website structures the product into three modules and six functional layers: Event Planning (AI concept generation, multi-option venue recommendations, LLM-based supplier search and structured RFPs), Budget & Compliance (real-time budget dashboards, approval logic with policy thresholds, preferred/blocked supplier management, CO₂ reporting), and Event Execution (participant RSVP, dietary and transport preferences, on-site task Gantt with AI coordination agents). The architecture is described as model-agnostic AI agents running 24/7 automated tasks, paired with human event experts for quality assurance and on-site support. The FAQ states a supplier network of 30,000+ covering hotels, conference venues, unique locations, caterers, AV/technical teams, ground transport, entertainers, and team-building partners. Ground logistics (shuttles, buses, private transfers, trains) are in scope; flight booking is explicitly excluded. Minimum event budget is €5,000; supported scale ranges from 5–10-person executive meetings to 500+ attendee company offsites. The site's 'By the numbers' section currently displays zero values (0k suppliers sourced, 0% average savings, 0 hrs time saved), indicating these metrics are either placeholders or not yet populated with verified data.

02

Users, buyers and demand

Payers are corporate event, marketing, HR, and sales departments plus procurement teams. The website explicitly separates two user roles: event planners (who write briefs, select concepts, and coordinate) and procurement (who manage approvals, budgets, and supplier compliance). Pain-point frequency: large enterprises typically trigger a 10+ person offsite or summit monthly to quarterly, plus fixed annual nodes (year-end parties, kick-offs), implying 6–20 procurement events per client per year, each involving comparison of 5–30 suppliers. The FAQ emphasizes that eventfirst is the 'single contracting party,' meaning procurement only onboards eventfirst once; all downstream supplier payments are handled by eventfirst. The platform exclusively serves companies, agencies, associations, and clubs—no private or consumer events.

03

The actual workflow

Input: the client fills a brief via the AI assistant (goal, headcount, location, date, budget cap). The system instantly generates three priced concept options (e.g., Cascais coastal retreat €52,400 / Sintra vineyard estate €48,900 / Lisbon city design loft €44,100—illustrative website data). Selecting a concept triggers LLM supplier search, returning comparable quote cards with service line-items (WiFi, catering, AV, parking). Binding quotes arrive within 24 hours for small events and 72 hours for larger ones. Approval routing uses a policy engine: requests under €3,000 auto-clear; above that, the requester completes a need assessment and routes for sign-off. In the execution phase, the team uploads a participant list; the AI auto-generates a personalized event website, rooming list, and centralized logistics view. On-site, AI agents run the task Gantt (stage setup, audio, soundcheck, accreditation, registration) while human experts handle exceptions. Deliverables: brief-to-executable plan, supplier contracts, participant management dashboard, and on-site execution briefings.

04

Pricing and unit economics

No pricing page or rate structure is publicly available. The FAQ implies eventfirst operates as a single contracting party, billing the client a bundled event fee while settling all underlying supplier payments internally; procurement teams onboard eventfirst once rather than managing dozens of supplier contracts. Inferred (unverified): revenue likely combines a per-event markup or percentage-of-spend model with a platform subscription for the enterprise workspace. Unit economics depend on supplier-side margins and event scale (€5,000 floor vs. 500+ person offsite), data for which is not disclosed.

05

Adoption evidence and gaps

The website's 'By the numbers' section shows zero values across all three headline metrics (suppliers sourced, average savings per event, hours saved per event), suggesting the platform is in early commercial rollout or that verified cumulative data has not yet been published. The only social proof on the site is a testimonial from Johannes Reck (Co-Founder & CEO, GetYourGuide), who is also an investor in this round—meaning the endorsement is partially insider-driven. No named enterprise customer case studies, logos, or reference lists are visible on the retrieved page. The team operates from locations in Berlin, Toulouse, Cologne, Almería, Budapest, Costa da Caparica, and Tbilisi, implying a distributed delivery model.

06

Competition and defensibility

Versus generic LLMs used for ad-hoc supplier search: eventfirst maintains a structured index of 30,000+ suppliers with historical contracts, compliance status, and CO₂ data, enabling the LLM to do matching and negotiation rather than bare search. Versus traditional event brokers (Cvent, Eventup, etc.): eventfirst bundles AI concept generation, an approval-policy engine, and on-site execution agents in one platform, whereas brokers typically handle a single category (venue or catering). Versus the old manual process: 24–72-hour binding quotes versus 2–4 weeks of multi-round broker negotiations. Barriers are conditional: supplier-network density across cities and categories (especially long-tail unique venues and niche AV), depth of integration with enterprise ERP/procurement systems, and the speed of accumulating on-site execution data (task completion rates, NPS, exception handling). If the 30,000 suppliers are largely legacy contracts rather than live competitive bids, matching quality degrades in long-tail categories.

07

How to read this round

The EUR 4.5M round (stage recorded as 'Other,' not a standard Seed/Series A label) includes General Catalyst, Booom, and Johannes Reck (individual capacity, Co-Founder & CEO of GetYourGuide). General Catalyst's participation signals institutional validation of the B2B event-ops software thesis; Reck's dual role as investor and testimonial author suggests early angel-stage involvement. Use of proceeds is not disclosed in the announcement or on the website. The next logical milestones: expand the supplier network from 30,000 to 50,000+, cover additional European cities, launch real-time competitive bidding and dynamic pricing, and publish at least three named enterprise ROI case studies. If no such public cases appear within 12 months, the platform narrative risks collapsing into a service-outsourcing story.

08

Where it could fail

(1) Supplier liquidity trap: if fewer than 40% of the 30,000 claimed suppliers are actively engaged and willing to accept LLM-structured RFPs, long-tail categories (unique venues, niche AV, specialty catering) will still require human brokering, undermining the AI-matching promise and degrading the customer experience to a slower, more expensive variant of traditional outsourcing. (2) Margin and scale mismatch: the €5,000 minimum event budget implies small engagements generate limited absolute revenue even with a markup model; if the revenue mix skews toward small executive offsites rather than 500+ person summits, gross margins may not cover the cost of maintaining a 7-location distributed human-expert team plus AI infrastructure, pressuring the unit economics of the platform model.

09

What you can take from it

For B2B vertical AI builders, eventfirst's approach is instructive: use a model-agnostic agent layer for structured search and RFP generation, but gate the last mile (on-site execution, exception handling, creative work) with human experts who have 25+ years of event-industry experience. The 'single contracting party' procurement model reduces onboarding friction for buyers—worth replicating in any B2B platform that intermediates multiple service providers. The visible gap: no public pricing, no named customer cases, and zeroed-out impact metrics suggest the company is still in early commercial proof stage; investors and prospects should treat all performance claims as company-reported until independently verified.

10

What to watch next

Over the next 6–12 months, verify: (a) whether the 'By the numbers' metrics populate with non-zero, attributable figures; (b) whether a dedicated pricing or plans page appears (currently absent); (c) publication of at least two named enterprise customer case studies with quantified outcomes (savings %, time saved, NPS delta); (d) supplier network growth beyond 30,000 and geographic expansion into new European or North American cities; (e) whether the approval-policy engine integrates with specific ERP/procurement suites (SAP, Coupa, etc.), which would validate the compliance moat.
