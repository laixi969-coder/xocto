FUNDING DESK · Italy · AI-driven B2B prospect research and personalised outreach, paid on results

SimpL

SimpL is an AI operating system for B2B sales teams that uses public data, a proprietary knowledge graph and deep learning to identify whom to contact and when, writes personalised LinkedIn messages and follow-ups, and charges only for qualified meetings that actually take place.

Work it replaces

It replaces SDR outsourcing and lead agencies, seat-based sales engagement tools, and the manual work of researching companies, writing openers and following up.

Business judgment

The key commercial bet is pricing on held qualified meetings rather than seats or subscriptions, which lowers the buying barrier but shifts delivery risk onto SimpL; profitability depends on meeting conversion rates and per-meeting cost control.

01

What the product does

According to the company site, SimpL combines company research, relevant signal detection, personalised message drafting, follow-ups and conversation management in one platform it calls an AI operating system for sales teams. Its documentation also exposes an Agent API and MCP interface so external AI agents can read the board, filter opportunities, generate and approve drafts, while actual sending is executed by a Chrome extension in the user's browser on LinkedIn. The product spans target setting through first touch, follow-up and reply; the site does not disclose the knowledge graph's data sources, industry coverage or accuracy metrics.

02

Users, buyers and demand

Users are B2B sales teams and reps; the payer is typically a sales leader or the company. The pain is finding companies and contacts worth approaching and writing messages that do not read like mass mail, a task that recurs daily. The site claims use by "the best" teams plus tens of other sales teams, but gives no customer names, industry mix or team sizes, so the customer profile is inferred from product design and lacks verifiable adoption evidence.

03

The actual workflow

The process has three steps: agree the target, offer, qualification criteria and price with the customer; the platform then researches companies against that target, surfaces outreach signals, drafts LinkedIn first touches and follow-ups grounded in concrete context, and the sales team approves before the Chrome extension sends and manages the thread. Billing occurs only when a meeting takes place and meets the pre-agreed criteria. The Agent API docs show draft states moving from pending approval to sent, with a readiness gate that blocks approval when the extension or LinkedIn is not ready.

04

Pricing and unit economics

The pricing page states billing per qualified meeting held, starting at €70 and able to exceed €150, billed monthly, with no seat fees, retainers or setup fees, no charge for no-shows, and rescheduled meetings billed only once held. This is company-reported pricing, not an audited conclusion. Unit economics: revenue equals price times valid meetings, while costs come from research, generation and outreach execution; if conversion is low or qualification criteria are loosened, margin erodes, though the cost structure is undisclosed.

05

Adoption evidence and gaps

Verifiable adoption evidence is limited: the site claims use by top teams and tens of sales teams and displays a funding announcement, but provides no customer list, retention, meeting volume or revenue data. The funding record shows a pre-seed round with Techshop Capital and Vento Ventures, which indicates financial backing only, not demand validation or product success.

06

Competition and defensibility

Versus general-purpose models, SimpL's difference is bundling a knowledge graph, signal detection, outreach execution and outcome-based pricing into a loop, plus an Agent API that lets third-party agents plug into its queue and approval flow. Versus the old process, it merges research, writing and follow-up into one interface. The moat holds only if signal quality and personalisation consistently beat direct prompting of general models and the per-meeting model stays gross-margin positive; otherwise a general model plus simple scripts can replicate the surface features.

07

How to read this round

This is a pre-seed round; amount and investors come from the deterministic layer, indicating an early validation stage. Use of funds is undisclosed. The next milestone should be proving unit economics under outcome-based pricing, such as verifiable qualified-meeting volume, customer renewal and per-meeting cost, rather than simply expanding the team.

08

Where it could fail

First, paying on results means SimpL absorbs the cost of failed outreach; if qualified-meeting conversion is below plan or customers loosen qualification criteria, gross margin can turn negative. Second, delivery depends on LinkedIn and a Chrome extension, so platform rule changes, account limits or extension failures can halt delivery. Third, if customers perceive the personalisation as still AI mass mail, renewals and word of mouth suffer; data and compliance responsibility is also not fully disclosed on the site.

09

What you can take from it

A transferable practice is tying pricing to the outcome customers actually care about, held qualified meetings, and controlling delivery quality with explicit qualification criteria and readiness gates; the Agent API also embeds the product in customers' existing AI workflows instead of forcing a tool switch.

10

What to watch next

Watch whether the site publishes verifiable customer cases and meeting volumes, renewal rates under per-meeting pricing, per-meeting cost and gross margin, and how LinkedIn policy changes affect the Chrome-extension sending path.
