Remanence

€27.30M

Tech.eu Funding Explorer ↗ Enterprise continual learning and post-training infrastructure for AI agents

Remanence is an AI company offering a continual-learning platform that uses real enterprise data and reinforcement learning to train, optimize and improve specialized agents and models for companies running agents in production.

Work it replaces

It replaces the current practice of optimizing agents mainly through prompts and tool harnesses while depending on frontier model APIs, as well as one-off fine-tuning that is never iterated.

Business judgment

The key commercial bet is that enterprises will pay to own a model that keeps improving from their own production traces, rather than continue renting frontier models per call; whether that holds depends on the training loop reliably beating existing baselines on customer tasks.

Deepgram

First discovery

2026-10-20 · AI transformation · Call centres and customer service / Healthcare / Media and content

What it is

When contact-centre QA, hospital transcription and media subtitle teams need to turn large volumes of recordings into text and analysis, they send audio to Deepgram's speech-to-text and voice-agent models, which return transcripts, call analysis and spoken replies; the new fact is that these offline batch jobs can run on AWS Trainium2 with eight model replicas per instance, while delivery quality and human review steps remain to be verified.

Why it matters in this edition

Trend: speech transcription is moving from per-minute cloud APIs to batch infrastructure that can be self-hosted and cost-tuned, so inference efficiency decides how many audio hours a fixed budget buys. Entry: avoid a generic transcription API and start from a compliance-heavy step such as contact-centre QA or medical transcription, charging per processed hour or per QA report, with domain vocabularies, de-identification and human review as the moat.

Real-revenue case

This Couple Solved Their Own Problem Built A Profitable Inventory

Founder interview, self-reported

Manual spreadsheets used to track products, materials, and the links between them.

What it is

A handmade-goods seller and her technical partner built an inventory system that tracks both finished products and the raw materials behind them, grew to 2,500 users on a free plan, then added paid tiers and reached $500 MRR within a month.

Real-demand verdict

Real demand · The founder was her own target user, stuck maintaining product-to-material relationships in spreadsheets because existing tools were either too expensive or missing that link. These sellers deal with material costs daily, so the need recurs.

Real-revenue case

ZenMaid Update: How We Grew To $120K MRR In 2021

Founder interview, self-reported

Manual scheduling and generic tools cobbled together to run a cleaning company's calendar

What it is

A scheduling SaaS built specifically for maid-service businesses, reaching over $120K MRR by July 2021 on the back of roughly 1,500 paying owners.

Real-demand verdict

Real demand · Scheduling is a daily operational need for cleaning companies, replacing manual planning and stitched-together generic tools; the payer is the operator himself, so the need is recurring and non-discretionary.

Real-revenue case

We Bootstrapped A Tech Talent Platform To $1.2M Sales In Our First

Founder interview, self-reported

Replaces unvetted marketplaces like Upwork and premium vetted ones like Toptal.

What it is

A freelance marketplace matching US founders with pre-vetted senior developers and designers in Latin America, earning on placement with transparent pricing; it hit $1M GMV in its first year of sales.

Real-demand verdict

Real demand · The founders were their own customer: Upwork doesn't vet, and Toptal's quoted $60/hr turned into $100+/hr, leaving a gap for vetted talent at a fair price. Buyers are founders racing to ship an MVP, so the need recurs rather than being a one-off experiment.
