What it is in one line

Hootsuite's AI social-copy tool: give it a topic and it produces platform-adapted captions, hashtags, and ideas in one pass, and can rewrite your best-performing old posts into new ones.

Who built it

Hootsuite (Canada, social media management SaaS, founded 2008) added OwlyWriter AI as an embedded feature in 2024, with an owlywriter.com landing site for funneling traffic. This is not a startup; it is an AI feature module of a mature product.

Caveat on the metric: the traffic board entry tracks traffic to owlywriter.com, and that domain also hosts Hootsuite's social-media strategy templates, case studies, and toolkits. So part of the 2.79M monthly visits is content-marketing traffic, and cannot all be attributed to the AI feature.

Read: this is the standard way a big vendor ships AI — not a new product, but a button inside the workflow users already have. Discount the growth signal, but the distribution advantage is real.

What it actually does

  • Multi-platform copy generation → one prompt produces versions for Facebook, Instagram, X, TikTok, with tone control (casual / funny / professional)
  • Rewriting top posts → turns your best historical posts into new versions for the same audience
  • Content repurposing → breaks blog posts and long content into social captions
  • Ideas and planning → holiday, launch, and trend templates; content calendar and scheduling
  • Image generation → visuals generated inside Hootsuite (added in the OwlyGPT iteration)

No standalone app, no public API — everything lives inside the Hootsuite platform. Outside Hootsuite, it is just a landing page.

What old behavior it replaces

Writing a separate caption for each platform.

Publishing one product message across five platforms used to mean writing five versions, adding hashtags, sourcing images, and then figuring out next week's content — half an hour per caption, scheduling measured in days; bigger brands outsourced it entirely. OwlyWriter compresses "one version per platform + what to post next week + headline tweaks" into a few prompts, while scheduling stays in Hootsuite.

Read: it does not replace writing ability; it replaces the organization of writing — turning the social operator from a writer into an editor: AI drafts, a human picks, scheduling continues unchanged. For teams already on Hootsuite, the migration cost is near zero.

Business model

Not sold separately. OwlyWriter is bundled into Hootsuite subscriptions: Professional roughly $99-149/month (one seat), Team $249/month (three seats, 20 accounts), Enterprise custom; 30-day free trial. Its job is to lift retention and average revenue on the main subscription, not to earn independently.

Hard numbers

  • traffic board figure: 2.79M monthly visits, +49.37% MoM, on the global growth board (including content-site traffic)
  • Pricing anchor: Hootsuite Professional $99-149/month, Team from $249/month
  • Hootsuite overall reputation: G2 4.2/5 (5,500+ reviews), Capterra 4.4/5 — platform scores, not OwlyWriter-specific
  • OwlyWriter's own user count, usage rate, standalone revenue: not disclosed — it publishes nothing independent of the platform
  • Comparison: standalone peers (FeedHive, Predis.ai, etc.) cost $19-59/month; OwlyWriter's "price" is the Hootsuite entry ticket

The call

Worth watching, with the traffic discounted. The +49% MoM is real, but 2.79M mixes in content-site traffic, so true AI-feature reach is lower; and OwlyWriter discloses nothing standalone.

Its meaning is as a demonstration of how AI features get eaten by mature products: not sold separately, no API, just a button inside Hootsuite, using AI to raise retention on the main subscription. Multiple reviews confirm the strategy works — for teams already on Hootsuite it genuinely saves time; for anyone not on Hootsuite it is expensive ($99-149/month minimum versus $19-59 for standalone peers).

Read: its competitor is not standalone AI copy tools; it is "whether a social team keeps using Hootsuite at all." Once AI captioning becomes table stakes, all social tools will be judged again on scheduling, collaboration, and analytics — OwlyWriter is a plus-point for Hootsuite to defend, not a killer feature for others to fear.

What to watch next

① Next month's MoM on 2.79M visits — whether content-site and AI-feature traffic move together ② Whether Hootsuite's reports or interviews ever disclose OwlyWriter usage or retention lift ③ Growth of standalone AI-copy tools (Predis, FeedHive) in the same window — if they grow faster, "button-ization" is losing to "sell it standalone"

What you can take from it

Product logic: the safest way for a mature product to add AI is "don't leave the existing workflow" — users already schedule in Hootsuite, so the AI lives next to scheduling. When adding an increment, first ask "where is the user's workflow today," and place the feature there instead of demanding they relocate for it.

Feature restraint: own only the social-copy step (generate, rewrite, re-version) and leave video and deep analytics to the platform. A feature module must be restrained so it does not upstage the host.

Positioning language: none. It is a tool page; nothing to steal.

Verdict

Worth watching. It demonstrates the standard play of a big vendor shipping AI as an add-on to an existing product, but the traffic metric is mixed and the standalone value is undisclosed. Watching it is watching whether the "AI as a module" route works.
