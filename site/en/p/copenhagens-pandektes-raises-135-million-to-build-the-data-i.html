01

Why this would be needed

Start inside the user's day · Public facts + workflow reasoning · 2026-10-06

Use case

When preparing legal opinions, litigation materials or compliance assessments, lawyers and legal researchers must locate citable authority in judgments, statutes and legal literature and confirm its current validity and provenance.

Subscribing to traditional legal databases for keyword search, then manually downloading judgments and statutes to read, excerpt and verify each citation.

Legal corpora are scattered across multiple databases and full judgment texts; retrieval relies on keywords plus manual reading, relevant cases are easily missed, and every citation must still be checked against the original text — slow work that cannot be skipped, and heavier in cross-jurisdiction research.

xOcto's call

Demand is evidenced

Trend: competition in legal research is shifting from model capability to structured supply of judgments and statutes, so whoever owns local-corpus cleaning and citation plumbing holds the entry point. Entry: start with a single jurisdiction such as Denmark, build judgments and regulations into a citable retrieval layer, and charge law firms per search or per case rather than selling a generic chatbot.

Reason to use it

Why users would choose it

Inference: compared with keyword search plus manual reading, Pandektes first organizes judgments and statutes into a structured, citable data layer so results carry their source, cutting the repeated work of hunting for authority in the original text and confirming provenance; law firms and legal teams handling high-volume or cross-jurisdiction research would choose it when they need to locate citable authority quickly. Public materials provide no retention or repeat-purchase
