01

Why this would be needed

Start inside the user's day · Public facts + commercial validation · 2026-09-23

Use case

Hobbyists, parents or teachers at a home desk or in a classroom take delivery of a small biped robot that runs out of the box, use its preloaded interaction routines for demos and companionship, and use the open-source repo plus simulation training pipeline (MuJoCo/PPO, sim2real, ONNX export) for beginner programming and RL practice, ending with a physical device that stands and gets back up after a fall.

The old approach is buying other desktop robot kits and assembling/configuring them, using building blocks or coding toys for beginners, or only watching biped walking in videos and simulation; the material offers no direct comparison against these alternatives.

The pain supported by public material: desktop-scale biped robots have mostly stayed at the demo-video or expensive research-platform stage; hobbyists and teaching users had to assemble, tune and build simulation environments themselves before the robot could stand and walk stably, a high entry barrier with hard recovery after falls. Microduck front-loads that step via a $399 preorder, an open-source repo and a companion simulation-training repo.

xOcto's call

Demand is evidenced

Trend: large model companies are pushing model capability into low-cost physical hardware, moving AI companionship off the screen onto the desk. Entry: start from children's programming education or in-store retail demos, where buyers already pay for physical interaction, selling hardware plus curriculum or demo packages rather than generic companionship; the $399 price is public but subscription or content pricing is not disclosed.

Why users would choose it
