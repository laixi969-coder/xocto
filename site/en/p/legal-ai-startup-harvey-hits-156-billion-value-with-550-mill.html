01

Why this would be needed

Start inside the user's day · Public facts + commercial validation · 2026-09-15

Use case

Lawyers and in-house counsel handling contract review, due diligence, or opinion drafting work through batches of contracts, precedents, and internal documents, needing to locate key clauses, compare precedents, and produce a reviewable first draft.

Junior lawyers or legal assistants read and summarize each document by hand, aided by keyword search and template libraries; some teams use general-purpose LLMs for preliminary Q&A, but without legal corpora or traceable citations.

Such materials are voluminous and term-dense; reading them one by one is slow and prone to missed clause differences, and the hourly billing model makes clients cost-sensitive about basic review.

xOcto's call

Demand is evidenced

Trend: high-barrier, hourly-billed professional services like law are having part of their basic review volume carved off by AI workflows sold on output. Entry: avoid generic legal Q&A; start from M&A due diligence, insurance claims clause comparison, or contract first-pass review at small and mid-size firms, where the old process and deliverable are clear, and charge per document or per matter while keeping lawyer review inside the product.

Reason to use it

Why users would choose it

Compared with reading each document by hand, it merges clause extraction, precedent comparison, and draft generation into one action, cutting the page-turning and note-taking burden of first-pass review so lawyers only review the output; teams facing large contract or diligence volumes would therefore choose it under time pressure. This is an inference from product capability and task structure, not evidence of retention or repeat purchase.
