# US moves to cut China’s remote access to AI chips

On the heels of the Nvidia scandal , the Trump administration is preparing to crack down on foreign data centers that are fueling China’s advancements in artificial intelligence by allowing remote access to otherwise restricted high-end chips necessary for AI training. In a summary of the rule change, the Commerce Department said it was closing a loophole that previously allowed foreign rivals like China, Russia, and Iran to bypass export curbs on certain advanced computer chips by instead renting access to those chips from data centers in Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, and Brazil. US rivals could possibly use that access to train and run advanced AI models “capable of supporting military- and intelligence-related activities that could threaten US national security or foreign policy interests,” the Commerce Department said. Among threats, the US fears rivals could use advanced AI to develop "more effective weapons of mass destruction and advanced conventional weapons, coordinated cyber-espionage and targeted cyber-disruption, algorithmic theft, and weaponized disinformation.” Read full article Comments
