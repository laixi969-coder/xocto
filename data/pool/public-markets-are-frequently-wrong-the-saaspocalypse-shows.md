# Public Markets Are Frequently Wrong. The SaaSpocalypse Shows Us Why

By Richard Anton Panics and pile-ons are a familiar pattern in the public markets, with a herd mentality wiping value from companies in an instant. Trading can swiftly become a barometer of emotions, rather than a well-informed gauge of value. We have seen this play out with AI’s impact on software companies and the supposed end of the Software as a Service business model. The doom-laden talk of the SaaSpocalypse was noise, with little signal, and most traders were initially unable to distinguish between a company that will thrive in the AI age and one that will be decimated by it. The SaaSpocalypse didn’t reveal the end of software. It revealed the limits of public markets in judging its value. This pattern erodes trust in the public markets. For companies, being publicly owned may no longer be appealing, and founders considering an exit may not view an initial public offering as the ultimate goal. How the markets got it wrong Richard Anton, co-founder and general partner of Oxx.
