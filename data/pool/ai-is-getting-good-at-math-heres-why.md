# AI Is Getting Good at Math. Here’s Why.

In May, an internal model at OpenAI found a counterexample to Erdős's unit distance conjecture . Open since 1946. Many experts had tried to prove it. OpenAI found a counterexample instead. A month later, OpenAI published a proof of the Cycle Double Cover (CDC) problem , then solutions to ten more open problems by July. And a proof of Navier Stokes in September. Anthropic published a formalization of the fermat's last theorem , and advanced bounds on Reimann's hypothesis . The models are suddenly good at math. The question that interests me is whether we can predict what kinds of problems these models will solve. What makes a problem tractable for an LLM, and what do these solutions have in common? If AI solves problems differently from humans, identifying the difference should help us decide where to point it. The unit-distance disproof has the richest commentary, so let’s start there. Problem complexity Here is a simple mental model. To solve any given problem, you need a sequence of hints or insights with work between them.
