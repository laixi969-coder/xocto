# Sector Snapshot: Construction Robotics Funding Soars To $1.6B, Tripling Previous Peak

Construction companies are struggling to find enough workers. For startups, that means venture investors are pouring money into companies betting that robots can help close the gap. Companies spanning construction and robotics have raised about $1.6 billion this year through Oct. 29 — more than 4x the $389 million recorded in all of 2025 — according to Crunchbase data. The surge was driven by three companies that raised the majority of the funding. The broad trend: For contractors struggling to hire, the appeal of construction-oriented tech offerings is straightforward: They need to get work done and there aren’t enough skilled workers to do it. The AI-driven data center building boom is only adding to that competition for workers. Labor shortages remain the biggest cause of project delays, according to a recent survey by the Associated General Contractors of America and NCCER . Some of the technology is already at work on job sites. San Francisco-based Bedrock Robotics , this year’s largest funding recipient in the sector, said in August that excavators equipped with its autonomous systems were operating without human operators on commercial projects in Texas and Nevada.
