# The Art of The Terrible Startup Name

When thinking of startup names, the usual question is how one identifies a great one . But perhaps equally crucial is the reverse consideration: What makes for a truly awful startup name? It’s a somewhat subjective exercise. Nonetheless, branding experts and occasional writers on the subject can agree on a few basic parameters. These include hard-sounding names, brands with negative connotations, and a certain ubiquitous suffix that could soon sound irrelevant. Below, we look at each of these categories in more detail No. 1: Harsh-sounding names One branding principle is that startups should avoid names that are unpleasant to pronounce. This includes words that sound too harsh or guttural, or are awkward in the native languages of potential users. Unfortunately, it’s a principle often ignored. For Athol Foden of naming consultancy Brighter Naming , the most prominent unpleasant brand name of recent quarters is Grok , the chatbot of Elon Musk ’s SpaceXSI . Foden hates it so much he devoted an entire essay to what’s wrong with it. “I still want to throw up when you say that name… and it is even worse in foreign languages,” he said in an interview.
