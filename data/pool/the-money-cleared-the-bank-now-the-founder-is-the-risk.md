# The Money Cleared The Bank. Now The Founder Is The Risk.

By Kelly Meerbott The round closes. The announcement goes out. For about a week, everyone is happy. Then the pressure doubles. The number that used to be a hope is now a promise. The board that used to be patient now has a timeline. And the founder who just finished the hardest sales job of their life is expected to make the best decisions of their life, immediately, on less sleep than they’ve had in months. I’ve spent 17 years coaching about 1,500 leaders, including venture-backed founders. The pattern repeats often enough that I treat the first 90 days after a raise as a danger window. Founders don’t get complacent in that window. They get flooded, and most of them won’t say so out loud. What pressure does to judgment Kelly Meerbott of YOU – Loud & Clear. Neuroscientist Amy Arnsten’s review in Nature Reviews Neuroscience puts it plainly: “even quite mild acute uncontrollable stress can cause a rapid and dramatic loss of prefrontal cognitive abilities.” The prefrontal cortex weighs options and holds a plan steady. Notice the word uncontrollable. After a raise, a founder controls less than the cap table suggests: the hiring market, customer timelines, the board’s expectations.
