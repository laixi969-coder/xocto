# Researcher plants 2m canaries into the AI supply chain. A privacy disaster surfaced.

Researchers planted 2 million digital tripwires in requests sent through a sprawling network of AI services to find out what happened to sensitive information entrusted to them. What they discovered was an ecosystem that leaked highly sensitive data like a sieve. The exposure was found through an ingenious experiment designed to test if sensitive information can leak through the vast chain of widely used AI services and how that information is exploited when it does. Many of today’s AI apps use services such as OpenRouter or Vercel that act as brokers. These “AI routers,” sometimes also known as “AI gateways,” receive large numbers of prompts sent from AI app end users and route them to an available inference engine based on criteria such as the one charging the lowest price or being best suited for the given task. Instead of using Anthropic, OpenAI, or another big provider, the developer hires an AI router to find the most suitable model. Security breaches, prompt theft, and privacy violations To test for potential leakage, researcher HD Moore assembled a collection of Amazon Web Services access keys, functioning credentials tied to empty AWS accounts, passwords for services such as SSH, and unique URLs, DNS
