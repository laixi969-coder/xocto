# Your AI Startup’s Exit Is Being Shaped By The Deals You Sign Today

By Aaron Rubin and Greg Volkmar Valuations for AI companies have soared, and the companies are moving faster than ever. In the financings our firm has worked on so far in 2026, AI companies raised at median pre-money valuations up to 2.5 times those of non-AI companies from seed through Series C. Across the broader private market, Crunchbase’s Current Valuation Model estimates that the more than 1,900 startups on its Unicorn Board are worth about $2 trillion more collectively than their last reported funding-round valuations. When everything moves that fast, the terms of your financings and of your contracts for compute, data and distribution get set early and in a hurry. Some of those terms will later slow a sale, add cost and erode value. The key is making sure you take long enough to consider what a deal does to your exit before you sign. Your investors may have a larger say in a sale Take license-and-hire deals, where a buyer licenses your technology and hires your team instead of acquiring the company.
