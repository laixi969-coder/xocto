# The internet’s Wuhan moment

It is January 2020 all over again. Something is spreading fast. Most people haven’t noticed, and the people who have are worried. Software, the internet, and cyberspace in general are currently under relentless attack. PHP2Shell was a key plank to fall: PHP runs about 70% of the world’s websites. But the breadth of attacks is more comprehensive than that, and the exploits will keep growing. On the same day, Node.js, a popular JavaScript library, patched a critical flaw, and Microsoft said a Chinese state-aligned group had used a swarm of AI agents to break into accounts. The attackers have company. AI agents are spreading across the internet. Matthew Prince, who runs Cloudflare, a network company, reckons they will outnumber human users 1,000 to one within five years. My own agents already browse more than I do. The internet was built so that anyone could plug in. Increasingly, that "any one" is a machine, and some of those machines are hunting. Mr Prince sums it up: “We’re going to have a couple of years where every single week it’s going to feel like the sky is falling.” He is right. It will get worse before it gets better.
