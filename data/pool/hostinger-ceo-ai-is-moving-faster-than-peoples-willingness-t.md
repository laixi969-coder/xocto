# Hostinger CEO: AI is moving faster than people's willingness to use it

AI has made it easier than ever to build a website, launch a product, or even create a software business without writing a line of code. But while the technology is advancing at breakneck speed, people's willingness to adopt it is lagging behind. For Lithuanian tech company Hostinger , which is transforming from a traditional web-hosting provider into an AI-first platform for building and running online businesses, closing that gap is critical. I spoke with CEO Giedrius Zakaitis to learn all about it. From web hosting to helping people run businesses Hostinger started as a web-hosting company, as its name suggests. Zakaitis recalls how installing WordPress once required users to upload files, create a database, and configure everything themselves. Managed hosting subsequently turned that into a one-click process. According to Zakaitis, the company eventually understood that its role was really about making complicated technical things available to non-technical people. “Today, hosting itself is almost an abstraction. You don't really talk about hosting anymore; you simply expect things to run. Ten years ago, if you had an idea, the question was: can you build it?
