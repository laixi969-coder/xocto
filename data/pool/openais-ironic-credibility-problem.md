# OpenAI’s ironic credibility problem

Around the start of October, OpenAI fired three employees from its safety department. The employees said they were given divergent reasons for their dismissal: One was told it had to do with his role coordinating with METR, a third-party research organization that OpenAI had invited to investigate the Hugging Face incident . “I was told verbally I was fired because of the way I communicated with METR,” Tomek Korbak wrote on X . “No other reasons were given and nothing was put in writing. To be clear, talking to METR was my job.” Another was told something similar but vaguer. “I was told OpenAI no longer trusts me because I was speaking too much to third-party safety organizations, implying I leaked company IP. I never shared company IP,” Mikita Balesni wrote . And a third was told something quite different. “I was given one reason: that I accessed an executive’s email,” Jasmine Wang wrote . “OpenAI delegated that access to me for recruiting. When I no longer needed it, I asked IT to remove it. They did not action my request, I couldn’t remove it myself, and the inbox was combined in an indistinguishable way in my phone’s mail app.
