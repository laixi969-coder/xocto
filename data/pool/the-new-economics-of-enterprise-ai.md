# The new economics of enterprise AI

Imagine your company just discovered electricity. At first only a few machines run on it, and nobody worries about efficiency — consumption is low and the value is obvious. But once electricity becomes infrastructure, every process depends on it, and the question changes from “can we use it?” to “how efficiently are we using it?” Enterprise AI is going through the same shift. What began as isolated chatbot and copilot experiments is becoming a foundational layer of the business, with models, agents, and data increasingly interconnected in production workloads. The important question stops being what a token or GPU-hour costs. It becomes how efficiently the AI environment converts paid capacity into useful business outcomes. That’s the metric worth tracking: effective cost per useful outcome — the total platform, model, operations, and capacity cost required to complete work the business can actually use, whether a resolved request or a completed workflow. Three ways to run AI Utilization is central to the economics. Directing more work to paid-for capacity lowers effective unit cost; idle GPUs, fragmented workloads, and oversized models all raise the cost of every useful result.
