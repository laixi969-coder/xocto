# Tech Race Summit participants warn of obstacles to turning AI pilots into profits [Sponsored]

Early in his talk, Georgios Chatzimilioudis, a Solutions Engineer at Oracle, asked the audience to raise a hand if they expected their business to use more AI in three years than today. Hands went up. He asked who expected to use more than one model, and the hands stayed up. Then he asked who was confident they knew which model, chip, platform or framework would matter most to their business in three years. Every hand came down. This set the tone for the inaugural Tech Race Summit , organised by SOFTSWISS, which brought together over 1,500 participants from fintechs, e-commerce companies, iGaming operators and other digital players facing the same pressures in high-load industries. Organisational gap in AI adoption Businesses worldwide face significant uncertainty, given the breakneck speed of technological innovation. Beyond just keeping up with the newest AI-based tech, they are also facing challenging organisational and strategic decisions about how and where to implement AI to ensure the biggest positive impact on the bottom line. Buying access to a model is straightforward, but giving it reliable company data, deciding what it can do and proving its work justifies the expense is harder. Sergey Khurson, Principal Solutions Architect at AWS,
