# ‘Weird And Maybe Uninvestable’: How Reach Capital Spots Its Venture Winners

Before James Kim evaluated education startups, he used their products in his classroom. The Reach Capital partner began his career as a high school math teacher with Teach For America after studying astrophysics at Yale . He later worked in college access and Yale admissions, and earned graduate degrees in business and education from Stanford before joining Reach. As a teacher, he was an early user of ClassDojo , an experience he describes as “game-changing.” James Kim, partner at Reach Capital. (Courtesy photo) His investment firm, Reach Capital, has also evolved. Founded in 2015 after spinning out of NewSchools Venture Fund , the San Francisco-based firm initially focused on technology for K-12 schools and districts. Its mandate has since expanded to learning, health and work, with investments in companies including ClassDojo, Cartwheel , Stepful and Replit . In August, Reach announced its fifth fund, a $265 million vehicle that brought its assets under management to nearly $1 billion. The firm, which has backed more than 180 companies, plans to invest in approximately 50 additional teams through the new fund, targeting pre-seed, seed and Series A rounds.
