# Startup Founders Are Using AI To File Patents. Here’s Where DIY Becomes A Liability.

By Michael Drapkin and Nathan Mutter Patents can help startups establish competitive advantage, attract investors and protect innovations. But the traditional patent process is expensive, slow and difficult to navigate, particularly for companies with limited resources. Michael Drapkin, founding partner of Landfall IP. AI is changing that equation. General-purpose tools can research existing patents, document inventions and generate drafts that look like patent applications in minutes. Handling the process themselves can seem like an easy way for founders to save money. But AI’s capabilities are jagged. AI can perform some tasks remarkably well while missing legal and strategic issues that determine a patent’s commercial value. An application can look sophisticated while providing little meaningful protection. For example, a patent’s claims define its legal boundaries and what you can stop competitors from doing. Mistakes involving claim scope may remain hidden until an investor, acquirer or competitor scrutinizes the company’s intellectual property. Nathan Mutter, managing partner and founder of Landfall IP. The question isn’t whether startups should use AI. It’s how much founders can safely do themselves and when legal expertise becomes essential.
