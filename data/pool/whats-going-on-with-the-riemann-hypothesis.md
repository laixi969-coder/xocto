# What's going on with the Riemann hypothesis?

Last week, news broke that a mathematician had used AI to help crack one of the biggest outstanding problems in math: a proof of the Riemann hypothesis . The news follows several other major breakthroughs in math that have either been enabled by or managed entirely by large language model-based systems, a development that has left the entire field of mathematics in a state of upset. It's too early to say much about the proof itself—mathematicians are still in the process of digesting it. But we can say a little about how the proof came about, and what it tells us about the state of math. Tackling Riemann The Riemann hypothesis dates from the mid-1800s and was formulated by (you will be surprised to learn) Bernhard Riemann. There's not an obvious way to describe the details of the actual hypothesis simply, but it has implications for the distribution of prime numbers. This gives it significance for areas ranging from number theory to cryptography.
