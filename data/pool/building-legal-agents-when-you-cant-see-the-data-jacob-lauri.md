# Building Legal Agents When You Can't See the Data - Jacob Lauritzen, CTO of Legora

Legora can’t read a single trace of what its agent does for a lawyer. The data belongs to the law firm’s clients. How do you build and test an agent when you can’t see its work? Jacob Lauritzen is the CTO of Legora, one of the fastest-growing companies in legal tech history. The team started in a Stockholm basement with ChatGPT, Swedish law, and a search tool. Today, a lawyer can use Legora to draft and edit Word documents, review 10,000 contracts in a single table, or run due diligence. The agent also coordinates work between lawyers, clients, and other agents. When Legora made its own acquisitions, the team used it for diligence. What we talked about • Why Legora resisted adding a model picker for years, and why it finally added one • Jacob’s estimate that models already do about 60% of legal work well, and why the rest takes a smarter model • How three engineers and three lawyers build test environments modeled on customers’ work, much like the “worlds” used in reinforcement learning • A citation problem where the model got it right 70% of the time, then 99% after one release. Legora didn’t have to change
