# UK to ease non-compete clauses, marking boon for startups and scaleups

The UK prime minister today confirmed that the UK plans to ease restrictive non-compete clauses in workers' employment contracts, which could include banning them, marking a boon for the UK startup sector. Speaking at the Innovation Nation Summit in Manchester, Andy Burnham, the UK prime minister, said: “I can confirm that we will legislate to make sure that non-compete clauses will no longer be a barrier to hiring for our most promising startups and scaling firms.” The move comes as the UK government looks to boost innovation and give the economy a boost. According to a video of Burnham speech, reported by Politics UK, Burnham said non-compete clauses “stunt innovation” and likened the rule change to the “Bosman ruling for the innovation sector”. The move follows a campaign by leading tech startups such as ElevenLabs and Synthesia to limit the use of non-compete clauses and extended notice periods. Non-compete clauses are a provision in an employment contract that restricts an employee from working for a competing business or setting up a new startup. The government did not specify the scope of the restrictions it is planning, but these are expected to be laid out over time. Trade body the Startup
