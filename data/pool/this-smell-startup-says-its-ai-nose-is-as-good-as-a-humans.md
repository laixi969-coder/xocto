# This smell startup says its AI nose is as good as a human’s

In a lab near Newark Airport in New Jersey, a team of around 30 experienced human sniffers are slowly working their way through every odor on Earth. Their careful notes—“nutty,” “peachy,” “marine”—are cross-referenced with a chemical analysis to create a rich set of training data for what AI startup Osmo calls the world’s first foundation model for smell. The company’s goal, says founder Alex Wiltschko, is “to give computers a sense of smell.” “Scent molecules are really the only language most species on this planet can speak,” he says. “Most species can’t communicate with sound or visually, so it truly is Earth’s original language. And it’s incredibly powerful for us, from a memory and an emotional standpoint. You can smell something once and remember it for the rest of your life. You can smell something on the street and be pulled back to high school.” In the last few months, Wiltschko claims, Osmo’s model has achieved “human-level olfactory intelligence.” The company’s definition of this is that the model is about as good as the average person at detecting and discriminating between smells, and about as good as a junior perfume designer at creating them.
