# When A Startup Sells, Its Employees’ Equity Plans Can Change Overnight

By Ron Honig Acquisitions are reshaping the technology industry. Venture-backed AI companies acquired 195 AI startups this year through late September , already exceeding the total for all of 2025, Crunchbase data shows. For investors and founders, such acquisitions can represent major financial milestones. For employees holding equity compensation, a transaction can also change personal financial plans they had expected to follow for years. A client whose employer was acquired in an all-cash transaction described to me the atmosphere inside the company during the weeks that followed: Employees repeatedly discussed the deal in office corridors, trying to understand what it meant for their equity awards, their jobs and their future. She compared the confusion to the atmosphere surrounding a major reorganization or a round of layoffs. The transaction offered the prospect of substantial financial gains. Yet employees were struggling to establish what those gains would mean for them individually. One acquisition, different outcomes Ron Honig, co-CEO of From-Honig Family Office. Employees at the same company can emerge from an acquisition with remarkably different financial outcomes. Some own vested shares. Others hold restricted stock units, stock options or performance-based awards. Their rights depend on grant agreements, vesting conditions and transaction terms.
