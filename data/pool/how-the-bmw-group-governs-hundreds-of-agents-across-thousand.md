# How the BMW Group governs hundreds of agents across thousands of developers

When hundreds of agents serve thousands of developers, enterprise governance can’t be an afterthought. At the BMW Group’s R&D, more than 12,500 developers work on approximately 500 million lines of vehicle software code. This software is augmented by the BMW Connected Vehicle backend, a cloud-based system of more than 1,300 microservices providing digital services for 24 million vehicles in the field. In regulated industry, the bar for (software) quality, stability, and governance compliance is exceptionally high. While research, frameworks, and guidelines now facilitate building agents, industrializing and operating high-quality agents in an efficient, scalable manner remains a significant challenge. Scaling agentic AI across a large organization cannot be achieved by isolated teams. It requires collective effort, locally driven experimentation, and active knowledge sharing across the enterprise. This creates a fundamental governance dichotomy: Development teams need the freedom to innovate rapidly, while the enterprise must enforce standards for safety, security, composability, and cost-efficiency. Without that balance, organizations risk a fragmented landscape of AI islands and costly duplicate work. This presents a real challenge given hundreds of globally distributed teams building agents across the BMW Group.
