# When Traction Becomes Personal Exposure: What The Joonko Guilty Plea Means For Founders

By Kyle Jeziorski Ilit Raz , founder and former CEO of the AI startup Joonko , raised roughly $27 million after telling investors the company had customers it never had and revenue it never actually earned. When an investor later asked for proof, she sent a forged bank statement and fake purchase orders. Last month, Raz pleaded guilty to federal securities fraud . She faces a maximum of 20 years in prison. Founders raising capital must sell investors on what their companies could become. But Raz’s case illustrates the personal stakes of misrepresenting a business’ achievements, and investors are increasingly willing to take those claims to court. Plaintiffs filed 15 AI-related securities class actions just in the first half of 2026, nearly matching the 16 filed in all of 2025, according to Cornerstone Research . The distinction between future potential and present-day facts matters when describing customers, revenue and traction. Where ‘puffery’ crosses the line into securities fraud Kyle Jeziorski of Founder Shield. The issue isn’t whether a pitch sounds optimistic or ambitious, but whether its factual claims mislead investors.
