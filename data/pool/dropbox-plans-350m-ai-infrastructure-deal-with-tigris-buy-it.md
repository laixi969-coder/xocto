# Dropbox Plans $350M AI Infrastructure Deal With Tigris Buy, Its Biggest Acquisition Yet

Dropbox announced Thursday plans to acquire Tigris Data for $350 million in a transaction the company told Crunchbase News would mark its largest acquisition since its founding in 2007. Founded by former Uber engineers, Sunnyvale, California-based Tigris is building a network of localized data storage centers that it claims can meet the distributed compute needs of modern AI workloads. Tigris has raised about $31.9 million since its founding in 2021, per Crunchbase data . Its October 2025 Series A brought in $25 million from lead investor Spark Capital , with participation from Andreessen Horowitz and Basis Set Ventures , which also led its seed round. Other earlier backers include General Catalyst . Co-founder and CEO Ovais Tariq told TechCrunch at the time of its Series A that the startup had grown 8x every year since its founding in November 2021. The planned deal would give Dropbox a storage platform built for developers whose applications run across multiple cloud providers. A push into AI infrastructure The acquisition news coincides with what San Francisco-based Dropbox describes as “a significant shift” in its AI strategy as it expands beyond storing and sharing files into selling the infrastructure that companies use to build AI
