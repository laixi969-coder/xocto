# Startups Are Picking Names That Can Work Even If The Original Business Model Doesn’t

Startup naming tends to be a cyclical endeavor. For a few years, founders might be enamoured of silly monikers, made-up words, and creative misspellings . Then the pendulum swings, and startups defer to simple names that describe what they actually do. So where are we now? Probably somewhere in the middle. Per a Crunchbase analysis of startups 1 founded in the past three years, companies attracting investor interest are largely opting for names that are both distinctive and vague. It’s a preference that reflects this era of fast-evolving AI, in which founders want room to adapt. Examples of this stylistic direction abound among the most heavily funded newer startups. There’s Also , which is of course a micromobility spin-out. Perplexity is a genAI company although you couldn’t tell that by its name. And then there’s Sunday , which makes household robots, in case that wasn’t clear from the name. “Reality is a lot of these companies, in seven or eight years they’ve done at least one or two major pivots,” Athol Foden , who runs the naming consultancy Brighter Naming , told us. That makes it logical to pick a name that can still work afterward. Running out of names
