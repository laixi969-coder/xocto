# AI may soon change what it means to be a theoretical physicist

About six months ago, Suvrat Raju, a professor of theoretical physics at the International Centre for Theoretical Sciences in Bengaluru, India, asked a frontier AI model to solve a physics problem. He didn’t ask because he needed to know the answer. He had actually solved this particular problem already. But he had never published the solution—which made it a perfect question to test how good AI was at physics. LLMs have been trained on the whole Internet, so if a physics problem has been discussed online, it’s likely the model already knows about it and may have memorized the answer. So a fair test of AI capabilities needs to focus on problems that have never appeared online. The model didn’t answer Raju’s problem correctly, but it did better than he expected—well, enough to get him thinking that the experiment should be repeated and formalized.
