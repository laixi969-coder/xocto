# I spoke to seven physics experts grappling with the AI revolution

Today’s article is sponsored by 80,000 Hours, a podcast that explores the transformative impact of AI. About six months ago, Suvrat Raju, a professor of theoretical physics at the International Centre for Theoretical Sciences in Bengaluru, asked a frontier AI model to solve a physics problem. He didn’t ask because he needed to know the answer. He had actually solved this particular problem already. But he had never published the solution — which made it a perfect question to test how good AI was at physics. LLMs have been trained on the whole Internet, so if a physics problem has been discussed online, it’s likely the model already knows about it and may have memorized the answer. So a fair test of AI capabilities needs to focus on problems that have never appeared online. The model didn’t answer Raju’s problem correctly, but it did better than he expected — well enough to get him thinking the experiment should be repeated and formalized. Along with a colleague, he set out to create a theoretical physics benchmark for AI.
