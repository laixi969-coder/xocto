# What To Measure When ARR Isn’t Enough

By Kenza Zayani In October, reports that OpenAI ‘s annualized revenue was running well below earlier estimates sent AI-linked stocks sharply lower and prompted a fresh round of scrutiny over AI valuations. It also reopened a question investors have been circling for a while: Is ARR still the right way to describe an AI business? The limitations have been understood for some time. ARR was built for software companies selling predictable annual contracts, which is only part of how most AI companies make money. Through the early part of the generative AI boom, growth was steep enough that few people pressed the point. The OpenAI episode has made it harder to leave alone, partly because it showed how much weight public markets now place on a single private company’s numbers. For founders, this is worth paying attention to now rather than later. A cohort of AI companies is reaching the growth-capital stage, and the questions they face in diligence are changing.
