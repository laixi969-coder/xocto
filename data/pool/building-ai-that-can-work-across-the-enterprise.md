# Building AI that can work across the enterprise

For years, enterprises have approached AI as a series of discrete experiments. Now, as AI agents begin to move into the workflows that run the business, that model is starting to look inadequate. Organizations moving from isolated pilots toward AI agents and autonomous workflows are confronting a deeper challenge: rethinking how data, business processes, governance, and people fit together, turning AI from a collection of tools into part of the enterprise operating model. For Thorsten Leiduck, SVP and global head of SAP Business AI Platform Ecosystem, that shift begins with treating AI as an enterprise platform rather than a collection of disconnected projects. “AI is absolutely dependent on the data foundation,” he says, arguing that companies need a connected, semantically rich layer that reflects their business before they can reliably deploy AI across processes. The stakes also change as AI moves into mission-critical workflows. For some everyday applications, “80% might be good enough,” he says, but in areas such as supply chains or financial close, that level of accuracy is “a very terrible result.” That foundation also determines which AI applications are worth pursuing. Satinder Pal Singh, associate VP North America for SAP BAIP and SAP AI Strategy at Infosys,
