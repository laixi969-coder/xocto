# Can Frontier Models See Past a Decoy? Elorian Tests Visual Reasoning

This month Elorian launched its first visual reasoning model , Glimpse . It’s being promoted as “a new category of visual thinking model: a system that reasons about the physical world from what it sees.” View post on X ↗ So how does Glimpse compare to both frontier models and world models in vision tests? A new benchmark called DecoyCount , co-released by Elorian, provides some answers. We quizzed CEO Andrew Dai on the benchmark, and also talked about Elorian’s positioning in a crowded models landscape. DecoyCount results Elorian emerged from stealth in April with $55 million in seed funding , co-led by Striker Venture Partners, Menlo Ventures and Altimeter Capital. Now, in partnership with AI data company Mercor , Elorian is announcing the DecoyCount benchmark, which tests whether language models genuinely count objects or rely on visual shortcuts and substitution heuristics. According to a paper accompanying the release , frontier models scored below 50% accuracy on DecoyCount — the highest was GPT-6 Astra, with 43.17%. Elorian’s new model isn’t mentioned in the paper, but Dai told us that Glimpse scored 50.48% in an internal test.
