# Agents Prompt People: Using Agents for Long-Horizon Financial Work

At AI Engineer New York this month, we sat down with Aman Singh , the lead AI Engineer at Arca , an “AI-native” company providing wealth management services. We discovered that while agents do most of the background work in the firm, there are guardrails in place to ensure that human advisors are making the final decisions. The goal is for AI to guide the process; or as Singh nicely put it, “agents prompt people more than people prompt agents.” Arca recently raised $48.5 Million in a round led by General Catalyst, with participation from Index Ventures and Venrock. One of the features of using AI in financial services, explained Singh, is that it’s typically long-horizon work — although not long-running. In his AIE presentation, Singh noted that agent tasks at Arca can take up to ten days. “It's very stop-start, there's many actors in between, many decision points, many handoffs,” he said. For this reason, Arca has developed an internal memory system . “So we have this persistence for every client ,” Singh said in our podcast discussion. “What has happened so far?
