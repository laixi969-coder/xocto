## What it is in one line

A webpage you can scroll through all 43,252,003,274,489,856,000 Rubik's Cube states — turning a number too large to picture into something scrollable, URL-locatable, and walkable like an infinite corridor.

## Who built it

Built by HN user Alen123, hosted on his own domain (everycube.alen.is), posted as a Show HN on August 10, 2026. No team, no company, no public GitHub repo — one person and one web page built on a whim.

_Read: a classic "one-night obsession" piece. The author disclosed no implementation details, did not open-source it, wrote no intro page — the work is entirely itself. That unexplained restraint is part of the project's character: all it wants is how it feels the moment you start scrolling._

## What it actually does

- **Scroll through cube states** → the mouse wheel moves you through the 43 quintillion states; several sequences exist (Permutation, Superflip, Checkerboard, etc.), each spanning a different number of states
- **URL-locatable** → every state has a URL/hash; refresh and you return to the same state
- **2D / 3D modes** → 3D is a rotating cube; 2D mode unfolds states into color swatches with color interpolation
- **FX effect** → a toggleable visual effect that smooths transitions between states

**The scale**: 43,252,003,274,489,856,000 — roughly 4.3×10^19. One commenter did the math: his scroll wheel covers about 12 states per inch of travel, which means even at light speed the wheel surface would need 9.5 years to scroll through them all.

## What old behavior it replaces

**It replaces nothing.** This is not a tool; there is no "people used to do X with tool Y, now they use this." What it replaces is a cognitive act — the number "43 quintillion" used to be something you read on Wikipedia and were briefly impressed by; now you can touch it with your own hand. It turns an abstract, unimaginable order of magnitude into something operable, explorable, and locatable.

_Read: if forced to name what it replaces, it replaces data visualization's oldest move — "draw the big number as a chart." It proves the ultimate form for a big number is not a chart but a passage: letting users walk through it hits harder than any single image._

## Business model

**None.** Free webpage, no ads, no paywall, no subscription, no company.

_Read: this is not a product failure; it is a category boundary. Projects like everycube and everyuuid.com belong to the same family — art/engineering spectacles for feeling huge numbers in a browser. Their payoff is reach and reputation, not revenue._

## The call

**This is data visualization taken to its limit, and it wins on restraint.**

**The transferable rule: let users walk through, not look over.** Most visualizations try to pack information density into a finite space; everycube goes the other way — the space is infinite, information density is near zero, but "hugeness" itself becomes the thing you experience. Scroll three thousand times and your hand aches; at that moment you have a bodily memory of 43 quintillion. Turning a magnitude into an experience lasts longer than any infographic.

**Why 285 points deserves attention**: HN is famous for seeing through things at a glance. A purely visual piece pulling nearly 300 points and over a hundred comments means it hit some universal curiosity. Commenters found "structure emerging from chaos" at specific state indices (actually simple patterns reachable in a few moves from solved), suggested showing the minimum move-count for each state — the work even made people want to add features to it. That is the signal of a good piece.

**Its limit is equally honest**: it is a one-off. No sequel, no extensibility, no roadmap. It is a single firework. For a business reader, its value is not "this could be a business" but "how does one person with no team and no budget create maximum reach with minimum resources" — that answer is worth more to keep than the webpage itself.

## What to watch next

② Whether clones appear ("scroll all UUIDs," "scroll all chess games") — tells you whether the form holds
③ Whether the HN discussion still echoes in a month (shares, remixes) — the aftershock of the reach

## What you can take from it

**Product logic**: if your product faces a number too big for anyone to feel (data scale, state space, possibilities), try making it a passage to walk through rather than a chart to look at. Scroll, zoom, locate — give users the physical sensation first, then tell them the number.

**Positioning language**: none. This work has no copy; its title is everything — "Scroll through all 43252003274489856000 Rubik's Cube states." Putting the most implausible number directly in the title is itself the hook.

**Pricing structure**: none. Free webpage, no business model.

## Verdict

**Worth watching.** A pure visual piece with no business model and no team pulled nearly 300 points, proving the "magnitude as experience" form has power. It teaches readers not how to build a business but how to make maximum reach with minimum resources. A one-off — keep its method, not its product.
