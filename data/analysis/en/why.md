## What it is in one line

A site that calls itself "a deterministic curiosity graph built on a probabilistic
model," whose public face currently consists of one daily WHY question.

## Who built it

rasheed106, under the company Ockams Inc. (the name is an Ockham's razor reference).
The public site why.com is extremely thin: the landing page has only "today's WHY" and
two example topic links (birthday candles, rome's collapse). The author disclosed in HN
comments that why.com/app is a separate, private desktop prototype focused on desktop
automation, scheduling, calendar, and email management, with "the direction of a fully
autonomous computer."

_Read: the public "curiosity graph" and the private "desktop automation agent" are two
faces of the same pitch — an elegant cognitive concept outward, an unglamorous desktop
tool inward. The gap between concept and product is the most suspicious thing about
this thread._

## What it actually does

- **Public site** → one question a day ("today's WHY") with sample topic pages; no
  real product function
- **Private desktop prototype (why.com/app)** → desktop automation, scheduling,
  calendar, and email management
- **Outward narrative** → a "deterministic curiosity graph," modeling curiosity as a
  probabilistic structure
- **Reply to HN questions** → the pain point is "cognitive overhead": always thinking
  about the next question, the next click, the next task. WHY automates that work

## What old behavior it replaces

Based on what is verifiable, the real target is "cognitive overhead in daily personal
tasks" — where schedules, calendars, and email used to require a human to keep pushing,
WHY's desktop prototype wants to execute them automatically. The public site's
"curiosity graph" looks like it aims to replace "scrolling feeds to find the next
curiosity."

But neither is verifiable today; this section can only record the author's own
description — "concept present, evidence absent." The public site has no signup entry
and no observable product behavior.

## The call

**This is a concept-product mismatch.** "A deterministic curiosity graph built on a
probabilistic model" is the loudest and least verifiable phrase in this entire thread.
Nothing on the public site supports the description — no graph, no interaction, no
paper, no technical note. Putting "deterministic" and "probabilistic model" in the same
sentence is provocative, but without an explanation it is just rhetoric.

**The truth the author revealed in HN comments is more honest than the landing page.**
why.com/app is a private desktop prototype doing desktop automation, scheduling,
calendar, and email management — the least scarce agent direction of 2026, which nearly
every large company is also building. Wrapping a generic agent in a "curiosity graph"
is tagging a red-ocean product with a cognitive-concept label.

**5 points and 4 comments mean even HN did not catch this story.** If the concept had
substance, the comment section usually fills with technical follow-ups. The one person
who asked got: "it's private, and it's early."

**The most worth-remembering thing is the author's own diagnosis of the pain point:
cognitive overhead.** "Always thinking about the next question, the next click, the
next task" — that is the human problem agent products should actually solve. It is a
pity it is buried under an unverifiable concept wrapper.

## What to watch next

① When the public site gets real functionality — "today's WHY" plus two sample topics

② Whether the desktop prototype why.com/app opens up — a private prototype that cannot
