## What it is in one line

Alibaba Qwen's international portal: chat, the model library, and API docs in one place — the front door for overseas users to Qwen, and the official site of the most-used open-source model family in the world.

## Who built it

Alibaba's Tongyi Lab. Qwen was led from late 2022 by Lin Junyang (formerly a Tongyi Lab scientist) until his departure in early 2026. This is Alibaba's international entry point, sharing one model family with the domestic Qianwen app across two sites.

**Note on entries**: Alibaba's domestic Qianwen app (star of the Spring Festival campaign, 203M MAU in February 2026) and this Qwen.ai international site (the 35.43M monthly visits traffic board tracks) are different things — the former is a Chinese super-app entry, the latter an overseas portal; a layer further out sits the open-model ecosystem (200K+ derivatives on Hugging Face).

_Read: Qwen's strength is three entry layers sharing one model base — the domestic app for scale, the international site for reach, open models for the ecosystem. Any single layer looks incomplete alone; together they make the "world's most-used open model" story._

## What it actually does

- **International chat entry** → overseas users chat directly on the web; models cover the Qwen3 family (flagship Qwen3-Max, over a trillion parameters, top-three on evaluations)
- **Model library and downloads** → weights, quantized versions, fine-tuning guides for the full Qwen line — the first stop for developers who self-host
- **API and development** → integration docs and platform for developers, competing with international cloud vendors on the same stage
- **Open ecosystem** → 200K+ derivative models, 300M+ downloads, surpassing Meta Llama as the most-used open-source model family globally

## What old behavior it replaces

Two kinds of people, two replaced behaviors.

**For overseas end users**: the habit of doing everything inside one closed chat tool. A free, open model with top-three capability became a mainstream alternative to ChatGPT — and the door anyone researching open models has to open.

**For developers**: the default of building open applications on Llama. Previously, Meta's Llama was the default for self-hosting an open model; Qwen replaced that default with download volume and derivative-model counts. That is not winning a leaderboard; it is winning a default behavior.

_Read: ecosystem replacement is the hardest to reverse — switching models means retraining, retuning, and re-releasing; once a team's stack sits on Qwen, the migration cost itself becomes the retention moat._

## Business model

Consumer chat is free. Monetization runs through Alibaba Cloud (Qwen models metered on the Bailian platform and international cloud), plus the cloud compute consumed by the open ecosystem. Alibaba does not disclose Qwen-specific revenue.

## Hard numbers

- traffic board figure (Qwen.ai international): **35.43M monthly visits, -2.85% MoM**, on the domestic, chatbot, and global boards
- Domestic Qianwen app (same model family): passed 100M MAU in January 2026, 203M by February, +552% YoY, third globally; Spring Festival DAU peaked at 73.52M, settling around 40M afterward
- Spring Festival campaign: 130M users, 200M+ orders placed with one-command ordering
- Open ecosystem: 200K+ derivative models and 300M+ downloads on Hugging Face, surpassing Meta Llama as the most-used open model
- Standalone conversions, developer registrations, and paid figures for the site: not disclosed

## The call

**Worth watching.** This is a "verified" stable leader: 203M MAU, +552%, #1 in open source — the data is real. The international site's 35.43M visits at -2.85% MoM is the normal cool-down after the Spring Festival dividend, not a problem — because Qwen's growth story was never about this one chat entry.

_Read: the real thing to watch is whether the three-layer structure feeds itself — open source feeds developers, developers bring cloud revenue, and the domestic app holds scale. In that structure the international site is the brand's face, not the whole business. The things to watch are continuity of direction after Lin's departure, and domestic retention once subsidies taper off._

## What to watch next

① Whether the 35.43M international-site visits return to positive MoM — organic growth abroad after the holiday dividend fades
② Retention of the Qianwen app after subsidies taper — how much of 203M was bought
③ Whether the open-model release cadence changes after Lin Junyang's departure — the next model generation is the test of strategic continuity

## What you can take from it

**Product logic**: one model base, three entries — domestic app for scale, international site for reach, open source for ecosystem — each feeds the others, and growth is never bet on a single door. For platform products, "one base, multiple entries, each with a job" is steadier than "one entry done perfectly."

**Open-source strategy**: the goal of open source is not reputation, it is replacing a default behavior — get teams to stack on your model, and migration cost becomes your moat. Judge open source by derivative-model count and team adoption, not by download leaderboards.

## Verdict

**Worth watching.** Real data, settled position, and a working three-layer structure. Track retention after subsidies taper and the continuity of the open-source roadmap, not the 2.85% MoM on the international site.
