## What it is in one line

Link-in-bio and appointment booking folded into one page: services, pricing, calendar, payments,
and reminders live behind a single link, so clients book straight from your social bio instead of
DMing to arrange a time.

## Who built it

Subhrajyoti Basu, an independent developer. His Indie Hackers post explains the origin: he
interviewed roughly 20 service businesses and kept hearing the same pain — the fragmented
Linktree-to-booking flow. The site is radiushq.cc and the product ran free during its early phase.

_Read: this is a "interview-driven demand validation" start — the founder is betting on a specific
pain he heard from real businesses, which is more honest than building a scheduler on a hunch. But
scheduling is an acknowledged red ocean._

## What it actually does

- **One-page storefront** → 8 draggable blocks (services, pricing, gallery, social links, text),
  no-code, roughly 10 minutes to set up
- **Direct booking** → clients pick a service, see price and duration, and book without an account;
  the claim is 30 seconds
- **Calendar sync** → Google Calendar integration: existing events block availability, cancelled
  bookings remove the calendar event automatically, and team members connect their own calendars
- **Automated reminders** → post-booking reminders reduce no-shows
- **Team scheduling** → shared service catalog, member management, fair-load routing for small teams
- **Guest booking + payments** → no client account required, payments handled in-page

## What old behavior it replaces

The way freelancers and small studios take bookings: a client lands on the social profile, DMs to
ask about price and availability ("DM to book"), the provider checks the calendar manually, then
sends a Calendly link or logs the slot by hand. On Instagram this chain is especially common, and
each booking can take several rounds of messages. radiusHQ replaces the "social profile + DM
confirmation + separate scheduling tool" trio, compressing the whole flow into one link. Twenty
interviewed businesses kept describing that flow, so the pain is real.

## Business model

**Freemium.** Solo professionals are free forever; paid tiers remove branding and add team
features; Team supports up to 10 professionals; Enterprise is custom (contact sales). The founder
says it is early and free while he learns from real businesses.

_Read: free solo + paid team is the standard path for scheduling tools, which also means fighting
Calendly, Cal.com, and SimplyBook.me on the same free-tier strategy._

## Four-way read

| Dimension | Call |
|-----------|------|
| Founder-product fit | Interview-driven validation; the founder has heard 20 businesses describe the pain firsthand — high fit |
| Product insight | Booking native to the bio page removes one hop versus a separate booking link — correct, but not disruptive |
| Execution quality | Two-way Google Calendar sync, team routing, guest booking — basic engineering is there; depth unverified |
| Timing | Calendly/Cal.com educated the market and locked the category mental model; a newcomer can only take segmented niches |

## The call

**Unproven. The angle is right, but the category won't let it take its time.** "DM to book" is a
genuinely painful, high-frequency problem for small service businesses, and removing one hop by
putting booking into the bio reduces real friction. But scheduling's mental model is already
claimed by names like Calendly — the default customer behavior is "send me a Calendly link," not
"check your bio page."

**This product is competing for a "default tool of a niche" slot.** Coaches, trainers, and
photographers who live on social acquisition might be captured in regions or scenarios where the
Calendly mental model hasn't formed yet. Survival depends on becoming "the default" within one
group of people.

## What to watch next

① Free-to-paid conversion — when the "learning period free" ends and whether paying customers exist
② Whether vertical real-world use cases (coaches/photographers/studios) appear
③ Adoption after the team tier ships — team routing is the only hard feature distinguishing it
from single-link tools

## What you can take from it

**Product logic**: interview 20 target users, repeat the workflow pain in their own words ("DM to
book"), then position the product exactly at the breaking point of that flow — the most reliable
source of demand for a tool. Also worth copying: place the booking action inside a page users
already live on (the bio) instead of asking them to build a new habit (opening a booking app).

**Positioning language**: none. Standard product copy; nothing to steal.

**Pricing structure**: free personal tier for growth, team tier for revenue, Enterprise through
sales — the textbook ladder for scheduling/collaboration tools. Directly reusable.

## Verdict

**Unproven.** Real demand, a reasonable wedge, but a red-ocean category with extremely thin early
data. Nothing yet shows it can break the Calendly-class mental monopoly. Re-check paid conversion
and vertical cases in three months.
