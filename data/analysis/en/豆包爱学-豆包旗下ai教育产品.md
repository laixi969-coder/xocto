## What it is in one line

The K12 AI tutor inside ByteDance's Doubao family: photograph homework for marking,
step-by-step problem explanations, essay coaching, an automatic mistake notebook —
grades 1 through 12, with core features free.

## Who built it

ByteDance. It began as a standalone product called "Hippo Aixue" and was folded into the
Doubao family as Doubao Aixue. It is the education vertical in ByteDance's AI matrix,
alongside Jimeng (image/video) and Trae (coding), with Doubao as the front door.

_Read: ByteDance made education a separate product rather than stuffing it into Doubao,
which says it believes education needs its own scene design and trust building — parents
are unlikely to hand "my child's homework" to a general-purpose chat app._

## What it actually does

- **Photo-question solving** → photograph a single problem or a full homework page; the
  AI explains the solution step by step, flags the knowledge points and common traps,
  supports voice follow-ups, and claims not to hand over answers directly
- **Homework marking** → covers mental math, fill-in-the-blank, multiple choice, word
  problems, reading comprehension, essays, and English grammar; marks a whole page,
  explains why errors happened, and files mistakes automatically
- **Essay coaching** → Chinese and English essays: angles, structure, material, polish,
  and scoring, customizable by grade
- **Mistake management** → mistakes auto-archived into a notebook and a knowledge graph,
  with recommended similar practice problems
- **Learning analytics** → parent and teacher views; core features are all free

## What old behavior it replaces

This used to be handled two ways: **paid** — hiring a private tutor by the hour (in
first-tier Chinese cities, hundreds of RMB an hour, with the parent supervising quality);
**free** — parents marking homework themselves, or legacy question-bank apps (Zuoyebang,
Xiaoyuan Souti).

Doubao Aixue occupies the middle ground: more professional than a parent (step-by-step
explanations, error analysis, variants) and cheaper than a tutor (free). The key
difference from question-bank apps is "does not hand over answers directly" — it positions
itself as teaching the thinking rather than providing the answer, a deliberate safety
design for both regulation and user trust.

_Read: this restraint — "teach the method, not the answer" — is the design decision most
worth copying in education._

## Business model

**Free, no ads, revenue not disclosed.** The official line is "core features have no
paywall." ByteDance is not treating it as a standalone revenue product; more likely it is
an education-scene entrance play: lock in parents and students free first, then convert
within the ecosystem (steering toward Doubao, or a future tutor-style paid tier).

_Read: education is the Chinese AI scene with the strongest willingness to pay, and also
the most regulation-sensitive — the policy red lines around compulsory-education tutoring
are real. Free is both risk avoidance and user hoarding; it is the optimal move for
ByteDance right now._

## The call

**ByteDance's move to occupy the education entrance with "free plus restraint."** In raw
numbers it is not in the general assistants' league (2.55M MAU in this pool), but it leads
the education vertical, and its presence on the growth board says momentum remains.
The -0.62% MoM is flat, nothing anomalous.

_Read: what Doubao Aixue really demonstrates is the viable path for AI products in China's
most regulated consumer market — not "doing the homework for the child" but "saving the
parent effort and teaching the student the thinking." Free is risk avoidance and
hoarding; if ByteDance ever wants to monetize, education is the scene in its matrix most
likely to collect money first._

**The risks are equally clear**: policy red lines on compulsory-education tutoring, and
the trust question of whether AI marking misleads a child. The official counter to the
latter is "no direct answers" — a correct product call.

## What to watch next

① Whether it stays #1 in the education vertical — the moat is parental word-of-mouth,
  not model capability
② Whether the official line discloses paid tiers or membership — what the first
  monetization move after "free hoarding" looks like
③ Whether regulatory messaging on compulsory-education tutoring shifts — one policy
  change re-prices this entire lane

## What you can take from it

**Product logic**: "give the method, not the answer" — in any scene where users want the
shortcut (education, job hunting, healthcare, legal advice), a direct answer means
enabling cheating and taking on liability; giving the reasoning, the process, and
self-check tools protects both user and product.

**Positioning language**: none. Official copy is feature description.

**Pricing structure**: core features free with no ads, trading free hoarding for a scene
slot — suited to resource-rich players buying a lane rather than chasing payback. Small
teams should confirm they have a channel before copying this.

## Verdict

**Worth watching.** The data is real and the product design is restrained and insightful
for the vertical, but this is a card-placement piece in ByteDance's education matrix, not
an independent business story. Watch it to see how ByteDance monetizes after "free
hoarding." Verify against official paid signals in three months.
