# AI product radar · 2026-09-30

## Today's Positive Direction

The clearest line today: **platforms are pulling the two layers of "entry point" and "identity" into the conversation itself**. OpenAI shipped mcp-extensions and sign-in-with-chatgpt-devkit together — one governs how plugins are distributed and presented, the other governs how users log into third-party apps. For vertical tools this is not one more channel; it is one less jump and one less signup — and it means neither layer stays in their own hands. Meanwhile the shopping entry point is migrating, capital-heavy industries are using software to extract existing capacity, and app builders are being absorbed into conversational agents. All three point the same way: **the opening is not another general-purpose entry point, but binding industry data, offline fulfillment capability, or existing accounts onto these new entry points**.

## Opportunity Roundup

### Platform Entry Layer: Plugins and Accounts Pulled In Together

### mcp-extensions

OpenAI opened an extension mechanism that lets third parties ship features as native-feeling ChatGPT plugins, submitted by developers and invoked by users inside the conversation. It changes how plugins are distributed and presented; review, billing and availability scope remain unverified.

**Judgment**: A vertical tool that only ships a standalone web page now carries an extra jump and an extra login. The opening is to package industry data or offline fulfillment into something directly invocable inside the conversation, rather than building another standalone app.

### sign-in-with-chatgpt-devkit

OpenAI offers a developer kit that brings ChatGPT account sign-in into third-party apps, with a local SDK, React components and design assets, so developers can add a login entry to their own products. It changes how accounts and identity are distributed; the exact permission scope remains unverified.

**Judgment**: As the platform absorbs the account layer, self-built registration loses value for vertical apps. The opening is to bind identity to existing industry accounts or offline credentials rather than building yet another general-purpose account.

### Market Context: The Shopping Entry Point Is Migrating

Three editor-confirmed market context items must be folded into today's roundup:

- **63% of consumers now begin product research with AI**, shifting the shopping entry point away from traditional search and e-commerce pages toward AI assistants. If that figure holds, how brands acquire traffic and how retailers structure product information, placements and conversion paths all get rebuilt.
- **Amazon blocked Meta's Muse AI assistant**, opening a new standoff over agentic shopping, while also launching an AI Agent for sellers and shipping Branded C at Unboxed 2026 (source truncated). The platform is walling off outside agents while entering the agent game itself.
- **Advertisers can directly influence Amazon Alexa's responses**, meaning voice assistants are folding ad delivery into generative answers and turning conversational assistants from pure information tools into commercializable ad surfaces.

**Judgment**: Together these say the monetization path for AI shopping and agentic commerce is taking shape, but the entry points are held by a few platforms. The opening is more likely on the supply side — structuring product information and fulfillment data — than in building another shopping front end.

### Capital-Heavy Industries: Using AI Software to Extract Existing Capacity

### Aglocell

When carrier RAN teams plan capacity on existing spectrum and base stations, they traditionally rely on drive tests, parameter tuning and manual experience to squeeze out headroom; Aglocell's AI software ingests RAN operating data and proposes optimization actions automatically, lifting capacity by 4.x% in trials (source truncated).

**Judgment**: The trend is that capital-heavy telecoms are using AI software rather than new hardware to extract capacity from existing networks — saving capex, not headcount. The entry point is high-value, low-frequency, strongly verifiable work like carrier capacity planning.

### Ainos

Fab facility or quality staff inspecting a line previously relied on human sniffing or lab testing to catch gas and odor anomalies; Ainos' AI electronic nose takes on-site odor/gas samples, has a model compare and judge them, and outputs an anomaly signal, delivering a checkable detection result that humans still confirm.

**Judgment**: Odor and gas sensing, once limited to veteran noses or lab submission, is becoming batch-deployable hardware plus models. Start with semiconductor, chemical and food lines that are sensitive to odor anomalies.

### App Builders Absorbed Into Conversational Agents

### Wabi

People who previously used prompts to generate small apps now describe needs in a chat, and Wabi generates interfaces on demand while carrying ongoing tasks, delivering a usable interface rather than a piece of code. Inputs, delivery boundaries and human confirmation steps remain unverified.

**Judgment**: App builders are being absorbed into conversational agents, with interfaces becoming on-demand output rather than a fixed product. Look at small teams that repeatedly stitch together temporary tools from spreadsheets and scripts.

### Arsaze

When video editors or content creators are working inside an existing edit and adjusting the timeline, they hand their editing intent to Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini, which directly modifies clips and order on the real timeline, producing a revised edit that humans still confirm.

**Judgment**: General chat models are becoming an operating entry point into editing software rather than another black-box video generator. Consider teams with heavy repetitive editing needs, such as ad agencies and short-video operations shops.

### Back-Office Repetition: Role-Scheduled Browser Agents

### ai-employees

A small-team operator or admin opens it when back-office data must be handled repeatedly: agents follow a preset schedule to log in, scrape, and fill forms in the browser, turning orders, inventory, or statements into deliverable spreadsheets or daily reports, which the user still confirms by hand. Role coverage and delivery boundaries remain unverified.

**Judgment**: The trend is role-scheduled browser agents replacing manual back-office clicking rather than yet another chat entry point. Start with fixed, error-intolerant flows like e-commerce reconciliation and freight booking lookups.

## Today's Conclusion

Platforms pulling in entry points and identity, the shopping entry point migrating to AI, capital-heavy industries extracting capacity with software, app builders absorbed into conversational agents — all four lines point the same way: **new value comes from binding industry data, fulfillment capability or existing accounts onto new entry points, not from building another general-purpose entry point**.
